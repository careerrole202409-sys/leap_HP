    0. Introduction

    Why a Simply Translated Site Fails in Thai-Speaking Markets

    Thai is spoken by approximately 70 million people — primarily in Thailand, where it serves as the national and official language. Thailand has a fast-growing digital economy, high smartphone penetration, and one of Southeast Asia's most active social media and e-commerce markets. Bangkok in particular is a regional hub for international business, tourism, and digital commerce.
    Building a Thai-language website presents unique technical challenges: Thai script has no spaces between words, uses a complex tonal writing system, and requires specific fonts and rendering configurations. Thai also has strong digital platform preferences — Line (not WhatsApp) dominates messaging, and Lazada/Shopee dominate e-commerce.
    This guide covers the technical, cultural, and SEO factors you need to build a Thai website that looks correct and performs well.

    1. Understanding the Local Internet Environment and Rules

    1-1. Writing Standards: Adapting Thai for Your Target Region

    Thai is relatively unified as a written language — Standard Thai (ภาษาไทยมาตรฐาน) is understood across the country. However, register and cultural context matter significantly:

    • Formal Thai (ภาษาทางการ): Uses specific formal vocabulary and honorifics appropriate for business and official communications. Business websites should use formal register — informal Thai in a business context can undermine credibility.
    • Polite particles: Thai uses gendered polite particles at the end of sentences — ครับ (khrap) for male speakers, ค่ะ/ครับ (kha/khrap) for female. In formal written Thai for business, these particles are used in customer service and CTA contexts to signal politeness. Consider carefully which particle to use consistently throughout your site.
    • Royal Thai vocabulary: Thai has a distinct vocabulary register for referring to the monarchy (ราชาศัพท์, rajasap). Never use common vocabulary when referring to the royal family — this is not just a cultural preference but has legal implications under Thailand's lèse-majesté law.
    • Thai script specifics: Thai does not use spaces between words. Typographical line breaks must be handled by word-segmentation logic — this is critical for correct text rendering on web pages.

    Set <html lang="th">. Thai script rendering requires Thai-supporting fonts — standard Latin fonts do not render Thai.

    1-2. Speed and Access Barriers: Handling Regulations and Third-Party Tools

    Thailand operates an open internet environment, but Thai script introduces specific technical rendering challenges:

    ❌ Critical Thai rendering issues

    • · Word wrapping without spaces: Thai has no word-separator characters — CSS must use word-break: break-word or Thai word-segmentation libraries (like Wordcut.js) for correct line breaks. Without this, Thai text may overflow containers or break mid-word at wrong positions.
    • · Thai font stacking: If a Thai-supporting font is not specified, browsers fall back to system Thai fonts that vary across devices — producing inconsistent rendering. Always specify a Thai-supporting font explicitly.
    • · Line height: Thai characters have stacking vowel marks above and below consonants — insufficient line height causes these to collide, making text unreadable.

    The word-spacing challenge is uniquely Thai — no other major language on the web completely omits word separators. This requires either relying on browser Thai text-shaping (modern Chrome/Safari handle this well) or implementing a JavaScript word-segmentation solution for precise typographic control.

    1-3. Laws and Licensing: Local Requirements to Check Before Going Live

    Thailand has an active and well-enforced data protection framework:

    • Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA / พ.ร.บ. คุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล), 2019: Thailand's PDPA, effective 2022, is modeled on GDPR. It requires lawful basis for data processing, explicit consent for sensitive data, a privacy policy in Thai, and data breach notification. The Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC) oversees enforcement. Businesses targeting Thai users must comply regardless of where they are located.
    • Computer Crimes Act (CCA): Thailand's Computer Crimes Act governs online content and has broad provisions against content deemed to damage national security, the monarchy, or public morality. Foreign businesses must be aware that certain content acceptable elsewhere may be unlawful in Thailand.
    • Lèse-majesté law (Section 112): Thailand's lèse-majesté law is among the world's strictest — any content perceived as insulting or defaming the Thai royal family carries severe criminal penalties. This affects any user-generated content features, social media integration, or comment sections on Thai websites.

    2. Content and SEO Strategies That Perform Locally

    2-1. Localized Content Creation: Trustworthy Information That Resonates

    Thai digital consumer behavior has been significantly shaped by the dominance of Line and the social commerce ecosystem:

    • Line as the primary contact platform: Line (not WhatsApp) is the dominant messaging app in Thailand — with over 49 million users in a country of 70 million. Line Official Account is the Thai equivalent of WeChat Official Account — the expected business presence channel. A "Add Friend" Line button is the Thai equivalent of a WhatsApp button in Latin America.
    • Line Shopping and social commerce: Line Shopping integrates with Line's messaging ecosystem for seamless social commerce. Thai businesses run significant commerce directly through Line.
    • Facebook still relevant: Facebook remains widely used in Thailand for business page discovery, particularly for older demographics and B2B contexts.
    • Thai keyword research: Thai search terms must be researched in Thai script. Use Google Keyword Planner filtered to Thailand — English keyword translations do not accurately reflect Thai search behavior.

    2-2. SEO Optimization: Targeting Local Search Engines

    Google holds approximately 97% search market share in Thailand. Thai-specific SEO considerations:

    • Thai word segmentation for SEO: Search engines segment Thai text into keywords using word-segmentation algorithms. Poorly formatted Thai text (e.g. with incorrect spacing or encoding) can confuse search engine indexing. Clean, well-formatted Thai content with correct encoding is fundamental.
    • Thai voice search: Voice search in Thai is growing rapidly — Google's Thai voice recognition has improved significantly. Conversational Thai keyword patterns are increasingly important for local and informational queries.
    • Line SEO: Line's search and discovery features are used by Thais to find businesses. A well-maintained Line Official Account with active Thai content appears in Line's internal search — complementary to Google SEO.
    • Structured data in Thai: Thai content supports the same structured data types as English — FAQ, Review, and LocalBusiness schema markup can improve click-through in Thai Google results.

    2-3. Choosing a Domain and Server: How Infrastructure Affects Your Rankings

    For Thailand-facing websites, Southeast Asian server location provides meaningful performance benefits:

    AWS Singapore (ap-southeast-1) is the standard choice for Thailand deployments — approximately 20–30ms to Bangkok. AWS Bangkok (ap-southeast-7, announced 2024) will provide the lowest possible latency for Thai users when available. Google Cloud Bangkok is also available. A CDN with Bangkok PoPs (Cloudflare has Bangkok presence) significantly reduces latency compared to European or US servers.

    Domain choice: .th is the Thai domain — specifically .co.th for commercial businesses (requires Thai business registration). .com is broadly accepted and used by many international businesses operating in Thailand.

    3. Design and Font Best Practices for Thai Websites

    3-1. Color Psychology and Cultural Meaning: The Right Colors for Your Market

    Thai color symbolism is rich and specific — with both traditional Buddhist associations and modern design conventions:

    • 🟡 Yellow/gold: Royalty, Buddhism, and Monday (each day of the week has an associated color in Thai tradition — yellow is for Monday, the birthday of King Bhumibol Adulyadej). Widely worn as a sign of royalty reverence. Gold has strong positive associations in both royal and Buddhist contexts.
    • 🔴 Red: The Thai flag's red represents the nation. Positive energy and power. Also the color of Tuesday in the day-color system.
    • ⚪ White: Purity and Buddhism. White flowers are offered at Buddhist temples. Clean white design aesthetics are well-received in modern Thai digital design.
    • 🟢 Green: Nature and fertility — associated with Wednesday in the Thai day-color tradition. Positive across most contexts.
    • ⚫ Black: Use carefully in consumer contexts — black has mourning associations in Thai culture, particularly for men's clothing during official mourning periods.

    3-2. Recommended Fonts and Sizes: Settings for Optimal Readability

    Thai script requires dedicated Thai-supporting fonts. Standard Latin fonts cannot render Thai characters.

    font-family: "Noto Sans Thai", "Sarabun", "Kanit", "Prompt", sans-serif;

    "Sarabun" and "Kanit" are purpose-built Thai web fonts from Google Fonts, widely used in Thai government and business digital design. "Prompt" is a popular modern Thai web font for consumer and startup contexts. "Noto Sans Thai" provides the most comprehensive Thai Unicode coverage. All are available via Google Fonts.

    Thai body text requires 1.8–2.0 line-height — Thai characters stack vowel marks above and below consonants, and insufficient line spacing causes these to visually collide across lines. This is the most common Thai typography mistake on non-native websites. Set body text at 16–18px minimum.

    3-3. Layout and Information Density: Design Trends That Resonate

    Thai web design has been shaped heavily by the Line ecosystem, Lazada/Shopee e-commerce UX, and the vibrancy of Bangkok's creative industry:

    • Line-centric layout elements: "Add Line Friend" buttons and Line QR codes are standard on Thai business websites — analogous to WeChat QR codes in China. These are prominently displayed, often in the header and footer.
    • Vibrant promotional design: Thai e-commerce design embraces bold promotional graphics, flash sales, and seasonal campaigns. Lazada and Shopee Thailand's campaign design — vivid colors, large sale badges, countdown timers — has set expectations for Thai online shoppers.
    • Royal and religious sensitivity in imagery: Images featuring the Thai royal family must be treated with the utmost respect. Buddhist temple imagery used decoratively (rather than respectfully) can cause offense. Verify all imagery with Thai cultural sensitivity in mind.
    • Mobile-first layout: Thailand has extremely high smartphone penetration. Single-column mobile layouts with large touch targets are the baseline requirement.

    4. Contact Options and Social Media Integration

    4-1. Building Conversion Paths: Contact Options and Social Media Channels

    Thai contact preferences are centered on Line in a way that is unique among Southeast Asian markets:

    • Line Official Account: The Thai equivalent of WeChat Official Account — a verified business presence on Line that users can add as a "friend." Enables direct messaging, promotional broadcasts, and the Line Shopping integration. Essential for any Thai consumer-facing business.
    • Line QR code: Displaying your Line QR code prominently on your website is standard Thai practice — users scan it to add your business as a Line friend. Far more effective than a contact form for Thai audiences.
    • Phone contact: A Thai phone number (+66) builds local trust. Thai consumers are comfortable calling businesses for inquiries and orders — visible phone contact is important.
    • Facebook Messenger: Still relevant as a secondary channel, particularly for older demographics and B2B contexts where Facebook Business Pages are used.

    4-2. Mobile Optimization: Adapting to a Mobile-First Society

    Thailand has high smartphone penetration — over 80% of Thai internet traffic is mobile. Bangkok users typically have fast 4G/5G, while rural Thailand has more variable connectivity.

    • Thai word wrap on mobile: Thai's no-space script makes word wrapping at narrow mobile column widths particularly challenging. Test Thai text rendering specifically on mobile screens — overflow and incorrect break points are the most common Thai mobile rendering issues.
    • Line in-app browser: Most Thai web traffic from shared links arrives through Line's in-app browser. Test your site in Line's browser specifically — it has its own rendering quirks.
    • PromptPay (Thai QR payment): PromptPay is Thailand's instant payment system — the Thai equivalent of UPI. All major Thai banks support PromptPay transfers. For any Thai e-commerce checkout, PromptPay QR payment is the dominant payment method and must be supported.
    • Tap targets: Standard 44×44px minimum — particularly important for Thai text which, rendered in smaller fonts, can create dense text areas that are difficult to tap accurately.

    5. Summary

    Checklist for Building a Successful Thai Website

    • ✔ Are you using a Thai-optimized font (Sarabun, Kanit, or Noto Sans Thai) with line-height of 1.8–2.0?
    • ✔ Have you tested Thai text word wrapping on mobile — particularly in containers and buttons?
    • ✔ Is a Line Official Account QR code and "Add Friend" button prominently displayed (the Thai equivalent of a WhatsApp button)?
    • ✔ Does the site render correctly in Line's in-app browser?
    • ✔ Have you reviewed all content for Thai royal and religious sensitivity?
    • ✔ For e-commerce: does the checkout support PromptPay QR payment?

    Thai script's no-space writing system, the Line-centric contact ecosystem, and lèse-majesté cultural sensitivity make Thai one of the more technically and culturally demanding localizations.
