## Quick Overview: What Actual Sellers Got Stuck On, and What Surprised Them

"I'm thinking about opening a shop on BASE, but what's it actually like?" is a question official step-by-step guides can only partly answer. This guide draws on real accounts from multiple people who've actually opened shops on BASE — the concrete build steps from account creation through design, how approachable the process felt even for beginners, the legal disclosure page that tends to be the first real wall, and the acquisition challenge that becomes clear only after the build is done.

## From Account Creation to Finished Design: The Real Build Steps

According to one blogger's first-hand account of opening a shop on BASE as an individual, the build process broke down into roughly five stages:

1. **Account creation**: Just an email address, password, and shop URL — one account reported finishing this step in "30 seconds."
2. **Entering shop information and payment methods**: Setting up your business information and accepted payment methods.
3. **Product registration**: Entering product name, images, description, price, and inventory.
4. **Choosing a design**: Picking from the available templates and customizing as needed.
5. **Publishing**: Flipping the shop to public, and you're done.

The blogger behind this account reflected: "even someone with no website knowledge like me was able to launch an online shop," citing the fact that you don't need programming or HTML expertise as one of BASE's biggest advantages.

## Approachable Even for First-Timers

A theme that shows up across multiple accounts is how low the barrier to actually operating the interface felt. One blogger, comparing BASE against other free shop-building services, rated it as having "more design and feature options," and chose BASE specifically to sell their own creative work (a self-published zine). Just filling in information along a template, and landing on a reasonably polished-looking shop, seems to lower the psychological barrier for anyone trying online selling for the first time.

## The First Real Wall: The Legal Disclosure Page

That said, a recurring friction point across these accounts is filling out the required legal disclosure (the Specified Commercial Transactions Act notice). It requires entering a set of details most people don't normally think about — name, address, contact information, return policy — and for first-timers, it's described as the step where people most often get stuck wondering "how much detail am I actually supposed to provide here?" There's a setting that lets individual sellers keep their address and phone number private, but some accounts mention only discovering that setting existed partway through the build process. Unlike design or product registration, the legal disclosure isn't something you can put off — it's a required step — so gathering the information you'll need (name, contact details, return policy) before you start building appears to cut down on getting stuck.

## The Challenge That Becomes Clear Only After Launch: Acquisition

A theme common across multiple accounts is the realization that "publishing the shop alone doesn't mean people actually see it." One blogger, while describing the build itself as smooth, wrote plainly that after launch, "acquisition is on you" — and that active effort, like running social media, is essential. Another account credited existing trust built with blog readers over time as the reason sales started right after opening on BASE, making the point that "there's a real person on the other end of the internet," and that who someone buys from matters.

What these accounts point to, taken together, is that BASE's usability and design flexibility make the build itself approachable — but how you bring people in after building is a challenge that BASE's own features can't solve for you. That part is on the seller.

## FAQ

**Q. Can you really build a BASE shop with no programming knowledge?**

A. Multiple first-hand accounts describe launching a shop with no website or programming background at all. Since the process is largely filling in information along a template, the build itself seems approachable even for someone trying online selling for the first time.

**Q. Where do people get stuck most during the build?**

A. A recurring theme across accounts is the required legal disclosure (Specified Commercial Transactions Act notice). It asks for a set of details people don't usually think about, and figuring out exactly how much to disclose is described as a common sticking point. Gathering that information ahead of time tends to make the process smoother.

**Q. Does publishing a shop mean it naturally starts selling?**

A. Based on these accounts, no. Multiple sellers describe hitting the reality that "acquisition is on you" right after launch. It's worth thinking through your post-launch acquisition plan with the same seriousness as the build itself.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

What comes through in these first-hand accounts is that building a shop on BASE tends to feel easier than people expect — but there's a mundane, unavoidable wall in the legal disclosure page, and beyond that, a much bigger challenge waiting: acquisition is on you. Getting too comfortable with how easy the build is can make the post-launch gap feel jarring. Anyone considering opening a shop would do well to learn from these accounts and plan not just the build steps, but how to actually bring people in once the shop is live.
