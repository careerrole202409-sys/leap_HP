## Quick Overview: What People Searching "What Is a BASE Shop" Actually Want to Know

Most people searching "what is a BASE shop" aren't after an abstract overview of the service — they want to know what's actually involved in building one: how fast, how cheap, and how good it can look. This guide touches briefly on BASE's basic mechanics, then focuses on the heart of the build process — choosing a design theme and setting up a custom domain. It also covers the acquisition wall you're guaranteed to hit once the build phase ends, so building doesn't become the whole story.

## The Basics: How BASE Offers No Setup Fee

BASE lets you start on its Standard plan with no setup fee and no monthly fee. Costs only kick in once something actually sells, through a processing fee and service usage fee — a "no cost until it sells" design. We cover the fee breakdown and when to consider upgrading plans in more detail in a separate guide, worth a look if you want the specifics. For now, the key takeaway is this: because you're starting for free, it's worth investing real effort into the two things that shape how your store looks and feels trustworthy — design and your domain.

## Building a Store That Looks Good: Choosing a Design Theme

BASE lets you build out your store design in three main ways: free themes, paid themes, and HTML editing.

Free themes cost nothing but tend to be simple, and you'll need to add any functionality you want yourself. They're a good fit for the stage where you just want to get a basic store shape in place without spending anything.

Paid themes are available through BASE's official design marketplace, typically priced in the ¥5,000-¥25,000 range. Many are built by professional designers, with layouts already designed around conversion rate optimization. Since you're starting from something close to a finished look, some say a paid theme is actually easier to configure for anyone without much design confidence.

HTML editing offers the most freedom, but comes with no technical support from BASE, and your page doesn't automatically receive platform updates — you're responsible for maintaining it yourself. Treat it as an option for people who already know HTML and CSS.

As a practical rule of thumb for the build phase: if you have a budget of roughly ¥10,000-¥20,000, a paid theme is worth it; if you want to get a basic shape in place for free first, start with a free theme.

## Building Trust: Setting Up a Custom Domain

You can start selling on BASE's standard URL, but if branding or SEO matter to you, setting up a custom domain is worth considering. The process comes down to three steps:

1. **Get a domain.** BASE doesn't offer domain registration itself, so you'll need to get one from an external registrar — and it needs to include a subdomain (e.g., shop.example.com). A root domain alone (e.g., example.com) can't be used, since CNAME can't be configured for it.
2. **Set up a CNAME record.** In your domain registrar's dashboard, set the CNAME value to "cname.thebase.in."
3. **Install the Custom Domain App from your BASE dashboard and set the domain there.** It can take up to 72 hours for the change to take effect.

The three steps themselves are simple, but the earlier you set up a custom domain, the less hassle you'll have later re-announcing a changed shop URL everywhere you've promoted it. It's worth deciding your domain plan during the build phase, before you start promoting your shop widely.

## The Acquisition Wall You'll Hit After Building

Everything covered so far — design and domain — is about the build phase: making a store that looks good. But no matter how good your store looks, BASE doesn't automatically bring in orders just because you've opened. BASE's own guidance states plainly that, unlike marketplace-style services, "you need to attract customers yourself."

It's common for sellers who put real effort into the build to be caught off guard by "I published it, and nobody came." That's not a failure on your part — it's a built-in reality of how the platform works, and one worth planning for from the start. It's worth putting as much energy into your post-launch acquisition plan — social media, visibility inside the PAY ID app, advertising — during the build phase as you put into design and domain work.

## FAQ

**Q. Free theme or paid theme — which should I actually choose?**

A. If you want to get a basic store shape in place without spending anything, go with a free theme. If you want a more polished result from the start, a paid theme in the ¥5,000-¥25,000 range is worth it — some say it's actually easier to configure if you're not confident in your own design sense.

**Q. Should I set up a custom domain early in the build process?**

A. It's not required, but deciding your domain plan early — before you've widely promoted your shop URL — saves you the hassle of changing it later. The setup itself is three steps and can take up to 72 hours to take effect.

**Q. If I nail the design, will the store sell without any acquisition work?**

A. No. Design is about making a good impression on people who actually visit — getting them to visit in the first place is a separate effort entirely. BASE's own guidance says acquisition is the seller's responsibility, so plan both your design work and your acquisition strategy together, starting from the build phase.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

The practical answer to "what is a BASE shop" is: a free foundation you can build real credibility and visual polish on top of, through design and a custom domain. During the build phase, choosing between free and paid themes wisely, and deciding your domain plan early, are the keys to a store you won't regret building. And don't forget the challenge waiting right after build-out: acquisition. Understanding that "building" a good-looking store and "bringing people to it" are two separate jobs is what prevents the post-launch letdown.
