## [Quick Summary] Shopify, BASE, or STORES — Which One Is Right for You?

One of the first decisions any e-commerce beginner faces is: which platform should I use? Shopify is the undisputed global leader in e-commerce platforms. BASE dominates the Japanese market with its beginner-friendly zero-cost setup, holding the top spot for online store openings five years running. STORES has been growing fast by offering unified management for physical and online stores. All three have their own distinct strengths.

This article cuts through the confusion with a fee, payment, cross-border, and customization comparison table, plus a decision flowchart tailored to your specific situation — helping you identify the right platform with clarity.

Here's the quick take: if you're testing the waters with monthly revenue under ¥100,000, BASE is the practical starting point. If you're focused on scalability and long-term growth, Shopify is the right fit. If you need unified management across a physical store and online shop, STORES is the strongest option. That said, there is no single right answer — the first step to making the right call is weighing your business scale, goals, and budget together.


## What Are Shopify, BASE, and STORES? The Position of Each Platform

### Shopify: Unmatched Scalability for Global Expansion

Shopify is a Canadian-founded e-commerce platform and one of the largest in the world, with over 1.7 million stores across 175 countries. Shopify Japan K.K. was established in 2017, and well-known Japanese brands such as GoGo Curry, Orion Beer, and Sanrio have all built their online stores on the platform.

Shopify's standout strengths are its high degree of customization and its robust cross-border e-commerce support. Standard features include multilingual and multi-currency support, international shipping configuration, and a rich theme library — all backed by an ecosystem of over 8,000 apps that cover everything a small business or large enterprise could need. Plans are available at ¥3,650/month (Basic), ¥9,900/month (Shopify), and ¥44,000/month (Advanced), all on annual billing.

One cost consideration: if you use a payment provider other than Shopify Payments, an external transaction fee of 0.2–2% applies on top of the monthly plan fee. Combined with the fixed monthly cost, Shopify generally becomes cost-effective once monthly revenue exceeds ¥100,000.

### BASE: Japan's Go-To Platform for Starting Without Upfront Costs

BASE holds Japan's top spot for online store openings five years running, with more than 1.8 million stores on the platform. Its biggest draw is the Standard plan — no setup fee, no monthly fee. For anyone who wants to "just try it" or minimize financial risk upfront, BASE is the natural starting point.

The pay-only-when-you-sell structure significantly lowers the barrier to testing a product-based business. The interface is simple enough for anyone to open and manage a store, even without tech experience.

The trade-off: the Standard plan stacks a payment processing fee (3.6% + ¥40) on top of a service fee (3%), which becomes a meaningful cost burden as sales grow. Upgrading to the Growth plan (¥16,580/month, annual billing) reduces fees — but at that point, switching to Shopify is also worth considering. Note that payment settlement takes 10 business days from the withdrawal request, which is worth factoring into cash flow planning.

### STORES: Japan-Built Platform with Best-in-Class Physical–Online Integration

STORES is built around one distinctive capability: unified management of an online shop and a brick-and-mortar store's POS system. The company behind STORES also operates "STORES Payment," a physical payment terminal — making integrated inventory and customer data management across retail and e-commerce a real differentiator that sets it apart from the competition.

Plans include a free tier and a Standard plan at ¥3,480/month (annual billing), with Standard dropping the payment processing fee to 3.6%. Templates are clean and well-designed, and no HTML knowledge is required to build a professional-looking store. While customization flexibility falls short of Shopify, STORES is highly regarded for its Japan-focused usability — particularly for businesses that already have a physical store and want to layer on an online presence.


## Side-by-Side Comparison: Fees, Features, and Cross-Border Capability

### Fee and Pricing Comparison

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Setup fee | ¥0 | ¥0 | ¥0 |
| Monthly fee | From ¥3,650 (annual) | ¥0 | ¥3,480 (annual) |
| Payment processing fee | 3.25–3.9% | 3.6% + ¥40 | 3.6% |
| Service fee | ¥0 (with Shopify Payments) | 3% (Standard plan only) | ¥0 |

Once monthly revenue exceeds ¥100,000, BASE's 3% service fee starts to add up noticeably. At that level, even factoring in Shopify's Basic plan monthly fee (¥3,650), Shopify often comes out ahead on total cost. The same cost comparison applies to STORES Standard — if you're not using the physical store integration, switching to Shopify becomes a viable option once revenue hits a certain threshold.

When it comes to customization and extensibility, Shopify leads by a wide margin. Its 8,000+ app ecosystem covers everything from email marketing and review collection to subscription selling and loyalty programs. BASE and STORES shine in ease of initial setup, but they fall behind when a growing business starts needing more sophisticated functionality.

### Cross-Border E-Commerce Capability Comparison

For small and mid-sized businesses exploring overseas expansion through e-commerce, cross-border capability is a critical selection criterion. Shopify holds a clear advantage here.

Shopify natively supports multiple languages and currencies, along with international shipping configuration, customs duty calculation, and international SEO. Displaying product pages in English, Chinese, Korean, and other languages — and accepting payment in local currencies — can all be done without custom development.

BASE and STORES are both designed primarily for domestic Japanese sales. Serious cross-border e-commerce on either platform requires integration with additional external tools. If overseas sales are part of the plan from day one, Shopify is essentially the only viable choice.


## Decision Flowchart: Finding Your Platform

Work through the questions in order to identify the right fit for your situation.

**Q1: Do you currently have any sales history from an online store?**

- Already generating ¥100,000+ in monthly revenue → Go to Q2
- Not yet, or monthly revenue is under ¥100,000 → Start with BASE (Standard plan)

**Q2: Is cross-border e-commerce (overseas sales) part of your plan?**

- Yes → Shopify (Basic plan or above)
- Not for now — domestic only → Go to Q3

**Q3: Do you have a physical store, and do you want unified inventory and customer data management?**

- Yes → STORES (Standard plan)
- No physical store — online only → Go to Q4

**Q4: Do you plan to actively use advanced features or marketing tools (email marketing, reviews, subscriptions, etc.) going forward?**

- Yes → Shopify (Basic or Shopify plan)
- For now, keeping it simple → BASE (Growth plan) or STORES

As the flowchart illustrates, there's no platform that's right for everyone. The best choice shifts depending on your stage of business and where you're headed. What matters most is pairing the right platform for today with a migration plan that fits your future.


## Real-World Cases: Platform Selection Patterns That Work

### Case 1: Orion Beer (Shopify)

Orion Beer Co., Ltd., the iconic beer brand from Okinawa, operates its e-commerce site on Shopify — including integration with Japan's hometown tax (furusato nozei) program and active international sales. Given the specific requirements around product descriptions and shipping conditions for alcoholic beverages, the company leverages Shopify's app ecosystem to handle these complexities. Managing domestic and international sales on a single platform helps Orion maintain brand consistency while keeping operations efficient.

### Case 2: Sanrio (Shopify)

Sanrio Co., Ltd., the globally recognized character brand, also builds its e-commerce presence on Shopify. Managing a vast lineup of characters across diverse product categories demands high extensibility — and Shopify's feature depth meets that need. As a global brand, Sanrio's multilingual requirements are also addressed by Shopify's built-in language support.

### Case 3: Independent Creators and Handmade Artisans (BASE)

BASE has been particularly well-suited to handmade artisans, artists, and independent creators launching small-scale online stores. Zero upfront investment, full store management from a smartphone, and a genuinely simple interface are what have driven its growth to over 1.8 million stores. For sellers with monthly revenue in the range of tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand yen, the fee structure stays within acceptable bounds — making BASE a high-value-for-money choice at that scale.


## FAQ: Common Questions About Choosing an E-Commerce Platform

### Q: Long-term, which is cheaper — Shopify or BASE?

It depends on monthly revenue. Under ¥100,000/month, BASE Standard (no fixed monthly fee) keeps overall costs lower. Once monthly revenue surpasses ¥100,000, BASE's 3% service fee starts to accumulate and will often exceed Shopify's ¥3,650 monthly fee. If you have a concrete plan to scale revenue, starting with Shopify from the beginning can save the cost and effort of migrating later.

### Q: I want to eventually do cross-border e-commerce, but can I start on BASE and migrate to Shopify later?

Technically yes — but migration involves real cost and effort. You'll need to export and import product data, customer data, and order history, reconfigure your domain, and rebuild your storefront design. If cross-border expansion is part of your medium-term plan, starting on Shopify avoids those future migration costs. Factor in the risk of temporary operational disruption during a migration before making the call.

### Q: Which platform is best for managing a physical store and an online shop together?

STORES handles this most smoothly. The integration with STORES Payment means physical POS data and online sales and inventory can all be managed on a single dashboard. It eliminates the double management of inventory and the fragmentation of customer data across systems, reducing the operational burden on staff. That said, if you're looking ahead to cross-border e-commerce or advanced marketing automation, also consider comparing against Shopify's POS app combination.


## Summary: Choose a Platform That Fits Both Today and Tomorrow

Shopify, BASE, and STORES each have clear strengths and well-defined target users. The shorthand — BASE for ease of entry, Shopify for scalability, STORES for physical-store integration — is a useful starting point for thinking through your options.

The final decision comes down to three axes: business scale, goals, and budget. In particular, if overseas expansion or cross-border e-commerce is on the roadmap within the next few years, considering Shopify early reduces the cost of migration later.

Choosing a platform is not the finish line — it's the starting point for building a successful e-commerce business. After the store is live, the real challenge is creating content that reaches your target customers, driving traffic, and converting visits into purchases.
