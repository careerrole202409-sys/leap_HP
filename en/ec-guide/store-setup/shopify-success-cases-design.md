### [Quick Summary] Design Principles Behind Shopify's Best-Performing Stores

Shopify is used by millions of stores across more than 175 countries. When you start building your own store, two questions come up immediately: what kind of design should I use, and which theme should I choose? This article examines representative Shopify stores — five based in Japan and five from the global stage — and unpacks the UI/UX elements their designs have in common, along with the themes powering them. Reading through these cases will help you build a concrete picture of what your own store could look like. Whether you're setting up your first store or a mid-sized business planning a cross-border expansion, there's actionable direction here.

---

### Before Reading the Cases: Understanding What Design Actually Does

#### Why Design Has a Direct Impact on Sales

Design in an EC context is not just aesthetics — it's the complete architecture of the experience from the moment a visitor lands on your page to the moment they click buy. According to Nielsen Norman Group research, web users decide within seconds whether to stay on a page. In that narrow window, your store needs to communicate brand credibility, product appeal, and ease of navigation — and that's what determines conversion rate.

One reason businesses choose Shopify is that this combination of design quality and functionality is achievable without an outsized engineering investment. But the default configuration rarely produces competitive differentiation on its own. The goal is to understand the design philosophy that makes successful stores sell, and then apply those principles to your own.

#### How to Actually Analyze a Store's UI/UX

When looking at store examples, it's easy to stop at "that looks great." Go further. Four specific questions to ask: ① What is the first thing the above-the-fold section communicates? ② How is the product page structured — what's the information hierarchy? ③ Where is the purchase path (add-to-cart) placed? ④ How does the store perform on mobile? Applying this analytical lens to each case transforms observation into something you can actually act on.

### 5 Japanese Shopify Stores Worth Studying

#### 1. Tsuchiya Kaban (Leather Goods & Bags)

Tsuchiya Kaban is one of Japan's most respected leather goods and bag brands. Their Shopify store (https://tsuchiya-kaban.jp/) is built around a composed palette of browns and beiges, and leads with high-resolution photography that captures material texture down to individual stitches — bringing the tactile quality of their products into an online context. Product pages include not just material and size information, but maintenance guidance, which builds in post-purchase support as part of the purchase experience. The brand concept of "crafted to be loved across time" comes through consistently across every element of the site. The theme appears to be custom-built on a Dawn-based foundation; the approach to large imagery and whitespace is well worth studying.

#### 2. CONVERSE JAPAN (Sportswear & Apparel)

CONVERSE Japan's Shopify store (https://converse.co.jp/) — the brand's Japan-market storefront — uses a strong black-and-white foundation accented with brand color, creating a simple but visually forceful identity. The homepage leads with large visuals of new arrivals and limited releases to maximize first impression. Product pages offer intuitive size and color selection, with content that updates as users scroll — a UX pattern that reduces exit rates. This store is a strong reference for mobile-first design in fashion EC.

#### 3. Francfranc (Interiors & Lifestyle)

Francfranc (https://francfranc.com/), a Japan-based interior and lifestyle brand, works with a soft accent palette of pinks and beiges that balances warmth with refinement — well-calibrated to their primary audience of female consumers. What makes this store distinctive is an in-site hashtag search function: customers can browse by tags like "#stylish" or "#solo-living" and arrive at curated product selections. It's a sophisticated move that ports SNS browsing behavior directly into the EC experience.

#### 4. Anker Japan (Electronics & Mobile Accessories)

Anker Japan (https://www.ankerjapan.com/), the Japanese operations of the global mobile charging brand, runs a clean, high-legibility layout. "New arrivals" and "bestsellers" are placed upfront, giving first-time visitors an immediate path to the most relevant products. Product pages lead with large images and essential copy, with full technical specifications reserved for users who scroll further — a two-layer information architecture that serves both quick decision-makers and thorough researchers. The prioritization of what to show first is sharp, and nothing gets lost in the layout.

#### 5. Nissin Foods Group Online Store (Food)

The Nissin Foods Group online store (https://store.nissin.com/) — Japan's iconic instant noodle brand — applies its signature red-and-white brand colors with total consistency across the site, producing a strong brand identity at every point. The way individual product brands like Cup Noodles and Chicken Ramen are organized with their own icons shows thoughtful navigation design for a company managing multiple sub-brands. Campaign and new-product banners are used to drive return visits. Despite being a major national brand, there's a lot here that applies to smaller EC operations with deep product catalogs.

### 5 Global Shopify Stores: Design Lessons for International Expansion

#### 6. Gymshark (Fitness Apparel, UK)

Gymshark (https://www.gymshark.com/) — a UK-based fitness apparel brand built on Shopify Plus — is one of the defining examples of global direct-to-consumer growth. Founded in 2012 by two students, the brand scaled rapidly through SNS marketing and community building. The store runs on a dark-toned design that projects the energy of the fitness category. Product pages are detailed, with multi-angle photography and full material information to reduce online sizing uncertainty. The brand's influencer-driven "drop" (limited release) marketing strategy is built into the site's information architecture — the connection between campaign mechanics and store design is instructive.

#### 7. Allbirds (Sustainable Footwear, US)

Allbirds (https://www.allbirds.com/) built a globally recognized brand around "footwear that's good for the planet." The Shopify store uses bold whitespace and minimalist design to keep attention squarely on the product. Each product page discloses the item's environmental footprint (CO₂ emissions) in numerical form — information design where the sustainability commitment directly becomes a purchase reason. The D2C storytelling that runs through the entire site demonstrates how to drive purchases on brand values rather than price, and it's a model that offers real direction to businesses trying to differentiate.

#### 8. MVMT Watches (Fashion Watches, US)

MVMT (https://www.mvmtwatches.com/) — a watch brand that originated on crowdfunding platforms — is widely cited as a textbook case of D2C growth powered by Shopify. The monochromatic design conveys the premium image the brand is built on. "Free Shipping & Returns" is displayed prominently and persistently throughout the shopping flow — a deliberate barrier-reduction technique. The integration with retargeting advertising is clean, making MVMT a useful reference for anyone thinking about the relationship between early-stage marketing setup and storefront UI.

#### 9. Pipcorn (Snack Food, US)

Pipcorn (https://www.pipcornheirloom.com/) is an organic snack brand whose store uses illustrated graphics and a warm color palette to put brand personality front and center. Product pages give significant space to ingredient origins and producer stories — an approach that speaks directly to the modern consumer insight that "who made this, where, and how" is a meaningful purchase factor. The subscription (recurring order) model woven into the product experience is also worth noting as a design for maximizing customer lifetime value.

#### 10. Seisuke Knife (Japanese Knife Shop, Cross-Border EC)

As an example of cross-border Shopify implementation, Seisuke Knife (https://seisukeknifekappabashi.com/) stands out. This specialty knife retailer, which has a physical store in Kappabashi — Tokyo's famous kitchen supply district — has built a multilingual Shopify store serving customers in Japanese, English, and Chinese. The store presents Japanese knife culture in a monochromatic, understated design that projects quality and precision. Product pages include detailed material information and use-case explanations in English to meet the expectations of overseas buyers. The brand voice — where the craftspeople's philosophy is communicated directly from the homepage — represents a design approach that maximizes what Japanese brands distinctively bring to cross-border commerce.

### 5 Design Principles the Best Shopify Stores Share

#### Principle 1: Consistent Use of Brand Color

Across every case above, you'll find a consistent color palette — typically a main color, an accent, and a background — maintained throughout the entire site. Color consistency builds brand recognition and trust. Nissin's red and white, CONVERSE's black and white, Allbirds' earth tones — each communicates "this is who we are" on first glance.

#### Principle 2: High-Quality Product Photography from Multiple Angles

The fundamental limitation of online shopping is that customers can't touch the product. Every successful store in this list compensates with generous, high-resolution photography from multiple perspectives. Tsuchiya Kaban's close-up stitch photography, Gymshark's on-model imagery, Jackery's lifestyle usage shots — each helps the customer answer "what will this look like when I use it?"

#### Principle 3: Information Design That Reduces Purchase Friction

Clearly displaying shipping cost, return policy, delivery time, and warranty terms close to the point of purchase is one of the most effective ways to prevent last-minute exits. MVMT's persistent "Free Shipping & Returns" display and EcoFlow's visible support access illustrate what it looks like to design "I won't regret this" assurance directly into the store experience.

#### Principle 4: Mobile-First Design

As of 2025, data from most major platforms shows that more than half of EC traffic comes from mobile devices. Shopify's major themes are responsive by default, but the stores in this list go further — they're genuinely optimized for legibility, tap targets, and load speed on smartphones, not just technically responsive.

#### Principle 5: Storytelling and Brand Content

Communicating not just what you sell, but why this product exists and what values the brand holds, has become a fundamental element of successful EC. The UL hiking philosophy of Yamatomichi, Allbirds' sustainability commitment, the craftsperson story behind Seisuke Knife — brands that articulate their "why" step off the price-competition treadmill and build a base of genuinely loyal customers.

### Recommended Shopify Themes: Characteristics and Selection Criteria

#### Free Themes: Dawn and Sense

Dawn, Shopify's default theme, is fast-loading with a refined, minimal aesthetic — widely used as a base for both Japanese and international stores. It works well for showcasing product photography prominently and is versatile across fashion, general merchandise, and food categories. Sense offers flexible section customization with a lighter, warmer color palette, and has particular affinity with cosmetics and lifestyle brands. Both are mobile-optimized and represent strong choices for operators building on a constrained initial budget.

#### Paid Themes: Prestige and Impact

Prestige is a refined, high-end theme popular among fashion, cosmetics, and jewelry brands. It delivers strong animation effects and full-screen visual storytelling capabilities — well-suited for communicating brand narrative. Impact is favored by fitness, sports, and lifestyle brands, with its bold typography and dynamic visuals. For operators aiming at the design direction that brands like Gymshark embody, Impact brings that aesthetic into reach without custom development. The most efficient path to theme selection: identify which case store design is closest to your brand, and find out what theme it runs on.

#### Three Criteria for Theme Selection

When you're stuck between themes, three axes help clarify the decision: ① Product catalog depth (if you're managing hundreds of SKUs, prioritize themes with robust filtering capabilities); ② Visual-led vs. text-led communication (match the theme's strength to your brand's communication style); ③ Cross-border plans (confirm whether multi-language and multi-currency support is built in natively).

### FAQ

#### Q1. When building a Shopify store, where should I start?

A. Begin by clearly defining who you're selling to, what you're selling, and how. Once that's in place, review the case stores in this article alongside your direct competitors to identify the design direction that fits your brand. After selecting a theme, use Shopify's free trial to build a demo store and pressure-test your assumptions. Prioritize getting the operational basics right — product listings, payment setup, shipping configuration — before perfecting the design.

#### Q2. Should I prioritize design or content in building a high-converting Shopify store?

A. Design and UX are more directly connected to short-term conversion (purchase rate), but content depth is essential for medium-to-long-term customer acquisition and retention. The two are inseparable. A practical frame: design earns the first trust; product descriptions, brand story, and blog content build the ongoing relationship. For businesses aiming at substantial scale, building an SEO-driven content strategy in parallel with the store build is the ideal posture.

#### Q3. What's different about building a Shopify store with cross-border selling in mind?

A. Three things to address first: ① Multilingual capability (product descriptions, navigation, customer support across languages); ② Multi-currency and local payment method support; ③ International shipping configuration and compliance with import regulations and duties. Shopify Markets makes it possible to serve multiple countries from a single store — customizing price, language, and currency per market. The important caveat: translation alone rarely converts well with international audiences. Localization — adapting value propositions, cultural tone, and SEO keywords to each target market — is what actually drives sales.

### Conclusion: Read the "Why" Behind Each Case, Then Apply It to Your Store

This article covered ten stores to identify the design principles that make Shopify stores sell, along with theme selection criteria. The goal isn't to replicate the look of successful stores — it's to understand the design thinking behind them, then translate that thinking into your own brand and product context.

Every successful store in this list applies five principles: brand color consistency, high-quality multi-angle product photography, barrier-reducing information design, mobile-first implementation, and brand storytelling. Use these as a checklist for your own store.

For businesses with cross-border expansion on the roadmap, localization becomes the critical additional layer. Translating a Japanese-language site into English doesn't reliably produce results with international customers. Rebuilding content to match the cultural context, values, and buying behavior of each target market — localization rather than translation — is what drives revenue in international EC.
