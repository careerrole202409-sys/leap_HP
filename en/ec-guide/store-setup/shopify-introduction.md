【1 Minute Overview】Understanding What Shopify Is and Why It's the Top Choice

Shopify is a cloud-based platform that lets you launch and run a complete online store for a monthly fee — no server setup, no programming knowledge required. Everything from product registration and payment configuration to storefront design can be managed from a single admin dashboard. Founded in Canada in 2006, Shopify is now used by over 2.3 million stores across 175+ countries, making it the world's #1 e-commerce platform by market share.

This article answers the question "what exactly is Shopify?" while breaking down the differences between Shopify and other major services (BASE, ColorMe Shop, MakeShop, and more), plus the key benefits and drawbacks you need to know before starting — all in a clear, comparison-table format that takes 5 minutes to read. If you're evaluating platforms for a new store, read on to the end.


What Is Shopify, Really? The Basics in Three Points

If you had to describe Shopify in one phrase, it's "an all-in-one tool for running an online store." Stripped of jargon, three points capture it:


Pay a monthly fee and get everything an online store needs — product listings, payment processing, inventory management, shipping configuration — all in one place.
No need to set up your own server or write a single line of code.
Manage everything from a browser, whether on a desktop or a smartphone.


More specifically, Shopify falls into the category known as "ASP-type e-commerce platforms" (Application Service Providers). BASE, ColorMe Shop, and MakeShop are in the same category — but Shopify stands apart in cross-border e-commerce capability and sheer extensibility.

How Shopify Got Started

Shopify was created by founder Tobias Lütke after he tried to sell snowboarding gear online and found the existing tools of the time simply too frustrating to use. The platform was designed from day one with actual sellers in mind. That philosophy carries through to the present, and a merchant-first approach to feature design remains one of Shopify's defining traits.


Why Shopify Became the World's #1 Platform

If there's one reason Shopify has spread as far as it has, it's speed and scalability. It dramatically shortens the time from zero to a live store, and the flexibility to add functionality as the business grows means the platform can grow alongside you.

A Design That Works at Any Scale

From individual sellers of handmade goods to D2C brands generating billions in annual revenue, the same platform handles it all. It's built for the "start small, scale up" approach. In Japan, major companies including Kose Corporation, JINS (Jins Holdings Co., Ltd.), and fashion brand STRIPE CLUB have adopted Shopify. Matsuya Foods also runs its official online store on the platform — and the breadth of these case studies speaks to its credibility.

8,000+ Apps for Unlimited Feature Expansion

Shopify has its own app marketplace (the Shopify App Store) with over 8,000 apps available. Email marketing, inventory management, review collection, loyalty programs — virtually any functionality can be added like a plugin. The ability to pick only what you need and combine apps to match your business model and stage of growth is a major differentiator from services with fixed, locked-in feature sets.

Cross-Border E-Commerce Capability That Stands Apart

Shopify supports over 50 languages and 130+ currencies, significantly lowering the barriers to international selling. Multi-currency price display, multilingual checkout, and international tax and shipping configuration are all available within the standard feature set. Most domestic Japanese platforms require separate tools or manual workarounds for overseas sales — the gap between Shopify and those options is substantial.

Three Key Benefits of Shopify

Benefit 1: Build a Professional Store Without Writing a Single Line of Code

One of Shopify's greatest strengths is the ability to create a polished, well-designed online store with zero programming. With 100+ themes (design templates, free and paid), you select one, apply it, and get a professional-looking site. Register product images, descriptions, and prices, and you're ready to sell. For deeper customization, Shopify's HTML/CSS editing and its proprietary template language, Liquid, enable fine-grained adjustments. The platform is designed to let beginners get started without specialized knowledge — then go deeper when they're ready — which gives e-commerce newcomers genuine confidence.

Benefit 2: A Smooth Path to Cross-Border E-Commerce

As mentioned, Shopify includes multilingual and multi-currency support as standard. The "Shopify Markets" feature lets you configure currency, language, pricing, and domain settings on a per-country basis — all managed from a single admin dashboard for multiple international markets simultaneously. For businesses that want to keep overseas expansion as a future option, choosing Shopify from the outset is a rational decision that avoids the cost of switching platforms later. Being able to start with domestic sales and step into international markets when the timing is right is a genuine competitive advantage.

Benefit 3: Diverse Payment Options That Reduce Cart Abandonment

Shopify supports a wide range of domestic and international payment methods: credit cards, convenience store payment, PayPay, Amazon Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, buy-now-pay-later, and more. Using Shopify's own payment system, Shopify Payments, brings the external transaction fee to 0% — an increasingly significant cost advantage as sales volume grows. Minimizing the "couldn't buy because my payment method wasn't available" scenario directly improves conversion rates.


Two Key Drawbacks of Shopify — Know These Before You Start

Drawback 1: Monthly Fees Are an Ongoing Fixed Cost

Unlike BASE, which offers a free plan to get started, every Shopify plan comes with a monthly fee. That fixed cost applies even in months with zero sales, which can be a financial and psychological burden in the early stages before revenue has stabilized. For individuals and small businesses especially, the "just try it for free" use case isn't well-served by Shopify. During the startup phase, it's important to evaluate the monthly cost against expected return on investment.

Drawback 2: Japan-Specific Features Require Extra Configuration

Because Shopify is a globally designed platform, fully supporting Japan-specific business customs — noshi (Japanese decorative gift ribbons), gift wrapping, cash on delivery (COD), detailed delivery date and time selection, and more — requires additional apps and configuration. Japanese-language support is available, but for complex settings and advanced customization, referencing English-language documentation is often necessary and takes time to get comfortable with. Compared to Japan-focused platforms like ColorMe Shop and MakeShop, Shopify's depth of Japanese-language content and completeness of Japan-specific features is more limited — something worth acknowledging before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. How is Shopify different from BASE or ColorMe Shop?

The biggest differences are cross-border e-commerce capability and extensibility. BASE and ColorMe Shop are optimized for domestic Japanese sales, with strong Japanese-language support and domestic payment integration as their strengths. Shopify, on the other hand, supports 175+ countries and 130+ currencies — making it a particularly strong choice for businesses with international sales in their future plans. Its app-based feature expansion also offers unmatched breadth, providing scalability that lets you stay on the same platform as your business grows. On cost, BASE's free plan option isn't available on Shopify, so for those prioritizing minimal startup costs, BASE often comes out ahead in the early stages.

Q2. Can a complete beginner really use Shopify effectively?

For basic store setup, product registration, and payment configuration, no programming knowledge is required. The admin interface is available in Japanese and is widely praised for its intuitive usability. That said, fine-grained theme customization and integrating custom apps does involve a learning curve. The best approach is to use the free trial to actually experience the platform and check whether it feels manageable. If support is a concern, engaging a certified Shopify Expert (partner agency) for initial setup is a realistic option.

Q3. For cross-border e-commerce, can Shopify handle everything on its own?

Shopify alone is sufficient to launch a multi-currency, multilingual store — but building a site that genuinely connects with local markets requires more than language translation. Content localized for local buying culture, search behavior, and design sensibilities is essential. Shopify provides the sales infrastructure; the marketing and local-language content that actually resonates with local users requires additional effort. Summary — Shopify Is Worth Considering as a Platform Built for Growth

Shopify significantly lowers the barrier to launching an online store from scratch. It enables professional stores without code, and its cross-border e-commerce capability is well ahead of domestic Japanese alternatives. The ongoing monthly fee and the extra configuration needed for Japan-specific customs are genuine drawbacks — but the cost-performance ratio tends to improve naturally as business scale increases.

For businesses focused purely on domestic sales, BASE and ColorMe Shop remain strong options. But for e-commerce managers and business owners who have "expand overseas someday" or "grow into a bigger business" on their longer-term roadmap, there is a rational case for choosing Shopify from the start.
