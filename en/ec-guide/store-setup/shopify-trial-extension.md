# How Long Is Shopify's Free Trial? Extension Tricks and the Latest Campaigns

## [1-Minute Overview] So How Long Can You Actually Try Shopify for Free?

Once you start looking into building an online store on Shopify, the first thing you probably want to know is how long the free trial actually lasts. Here's the short answer: Shopify's official free trial currently runs for 3 days. You don't need to enter any credit card details, and nothing gets charged automatically if you don't take action during that window. On top of that, there are periods when Shopify runs a campaign letting you use a specific plan for just 150 yen a month for up to three months after the trial ends. And if your goal is purely development work, using a Shopify Partner account lets you keep the build phase itself free for an unlimited amount of time. This article lays out exactly how the free trial works, what happens once it ends, and how to build your store while keeping costs down as smartly as possible.

## How Many Days Does Shopify's Free Trial Last?

Shopify's free trial currently runs for 3 days. The length has shifted over time — it's been as long as 14 days in the past — and campaign details get updated frequently, so it's worth checking the official site for the current number of days before you sign up. The trial clock starts on the day you register your email address and create an account. During those 3 days, you can try out the features of all the major plans — Starter, Basic, Shopify, and Advanced — and support is available in Japanese across every plan.

You don't need to enter any payment details during the trial. Unlike many web services that automatically roll you into a paid contract once the free period ends, Shopify is designed so that your store is simply paused once the trial ends, unless you've chosen a payment method and a plan. In other words, there's no risk of an unexpected charge from forgetting to cancel — which should be reassuring if you're running an e-commerce store for the first time.

## What You Can and Can't Do During the Free Trial

During the trial, you can build out your store in a way that closely resembles a live operation — listing products, choosing and customizing a theme, setting up shipping and payment methods, and installing apps. You can even register dummy products to see how your pages look, and switch between different themes to compare them.

That said, the free trial does come with clear limits. The most important one is that you can't actually sell products or run your store live on a custom domain. By default, a trial store either has a password set on the storefront or has checkout disabled, so ordinary customers can't purchase anything. To start selling for real, you need to switch to a paid plan. One reassuring point, though: everything you set up during the trial — product listings, design settings, and so on — carries over fully once you move to a paid plan.

## What Happens Once the Free Trial Ends

If you haven't selected a pricing plan by the time the trial ends, your account is automatically frozen — no special cancellation process required. On the other hand, if you did select a plan and move into a paid contract during the trial, canceling later requires you to manually pause or close your store from the admin dashboard. Understanding this distinction helps you avoid the surprise of "wait, when did I start getting charged?"

As for pricing after the trial, there are periods when Shopify offers a campaign letting you use a specific plan for just 150 yen a month for up to three months. That said, this kind of offer isn't always available and its details shift depending on the timing, so always check the official site for the latest terms before signing up.

## How to Build Your Store on an Effectively Unlimited Free Basis Using a Development Store

If you're looking to keep costs down while carefully building out your store, or if you're planning to have an outside agency build it for you, it's worth knowing about development stores through the Shopify Partner Program. Anyone can register a Shopify Partner account for free, and once registered, you can create an unlimited number of "development stores." A development store is a free Shopify account with features close to the Advanced plan — you can't actually publish products or sell live, but you can handle nearly all the build work, from theme setup to product listings to testing app functionality, at zero cost.

This is a method that Shopify-focused development agencies actually use in practice. A number of companies in Japan that work as Shopify Partners rely on development stores as a routine part of their workflow — Tsun Inc., which builds and runs the Shopify app RuffRuff, and non-standard world Inc., known for its guides on theme and app development, among others — and all of it is done through Shopify's own official mechanism. The typical flow is to build everything out in a development store, then switch to a paid plan and go live once you're ready to start selling — or, in an agency's case, transfer ownership of the store to the client. For small and mid-sized businesses considering Shopify, understanding how development stores work makes it much easier to get a sense of the cost and process involved when handing the build off to an outside agency.

Keep in mind that a development store itself has a password set from the start and can't be made public — it's meant purely as a free environment for building and testing.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a credit card during Shopify's free trial?**

A. No. You don't need to register any payment information when you start the trial, and nothing gets charged automatically if you don't take action during the period. You're only asked to choose a payment method and a plan once you're ready to actually start selling.

**Q. Once the free trial ends, do I definitely have to pay?**

A. If you don't select a plan by the time the trial ends, your account is simply frozen automatically, with no cost involved. You only move into a paid contract if you choose to keep using the service and select a plan. It's also worth checking for current offers before signing up, since there are periods when Shopify runs a campaign letting you use a specific plan for just 150 yen a month for up to three months.

**Q. Is there a way to extend the free trial myself, without going through an agency?**

A. You can't personally extend the official trial period itself. That said, if you don't plan to actually sell products yet and just want to build and test your store, registering a Shopify Partner account and creating a development store lets you keep building for free without worrying about a time limit. This is a legitimate method that Shopify development agencies themselves rely on in practice.
