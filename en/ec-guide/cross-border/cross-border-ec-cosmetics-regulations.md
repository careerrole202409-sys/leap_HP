## Quick Overview: Cosmetics Regulations for Cross-Border Ecommerce

When you sell cosmetics internationally, you need to check two sets of rules: the procedures on the Japanese side and the regulations of each country you sell to. If you simply ship cosmetics already on the Japanese market, Japan requires no special export procedure. Most destination countries, however, require advance notification, a local responsible party and labeling in the local language.

- **Japan**: No special procedure for exporting domestic products as they are. Translating labels or redesigning outer boxes requires a cosmetics manufacturing license and an export manufacturing notification
- **United States**: Under MoCRA (the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act), businesses must register facilities, list products and report serious adverse events
- **EU**: You need a Responsible Person in the EU and must notify products through the CPNP (Cosmetic Products Notification Portal) before sale
- **China**: Regular imports require registration or filing. Under the cross-border ecommerce retail import scheme, first-import registration and filing are waived, subject to conditions
- **South Korea and Taiwan**: A local importer must register with the authorities, and labels must be in the local language

This article explains each country's rules based on information from official government bodies. Information is current as of November 27, 2026. Cosmetics rules change often and decisions depend on the product, so check the latest guidance from each authority and consult a regulatory specialist before you start selling.

## Three Things to Check Before Selling Cosmetics Overseas

Before selling cosmetics through cross-border ecommerce, check three things: Japan's export procedures under the Pharmaceutical and Medical Device Act, the destination country's cosmetics regulations, and shipping restrictions. A product that sells without issue in Japan can still be stopped at customs, or be unsellable, if it does not meet the destination's ingredient or labeling rules.

1. **Japan's export procedures**: Whether you need a license or notification depends on the form in which you export
2. **The destination's cosmetics regulations**: Check advance notification or registration, local responsible parties, ingredients, and the language and content of labels
3. **Shipping restrictions**: Some cosmetics, such as perfume and aerosol sprays, cannot be sent by international mail

The line between cosmetics and drugs also differs by country. Sunscreens and whitening products sold as cosmetics in Japan may be treated as drugs or as a special cosmetics category elsewhere.

## Exporting Cosmetics from Japan: Required Procedures

If you ship cosmetics that are already sold in Japan without changing them, no special procedure is required under Japanese law. If you modify labels or outer boxes, that counts as manufacturing, and a license and notification are required. JETRO summarizes the rules by export type as follows.

| Export type | Required procedure |
|---|---|
| Exporting products bought from a Japanese marketing authorization holder, unchanged | No special procedure |
| Adding translated labels or redesigning outer boxes for export | Cosmetics manufacturing license + export cosmetics manufacturing notification to the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare via PMDA before manufacturing |
| Making export-only formulations or packaging from the start | Cosmetics manufacturing license + export cosmetics manufacturing notification |

Returns deserve special attention. Accepting a return from an overseas customer counts as an import (re-import), and you cannot accept it without a cosmetics marketing authorization license. Decide how you will handle returns in advance and state it in your store's return policy.

## US Cosmetics Regulations (MoCRA)

In the US, cosmetics generally do not need premarket approval from the FDA (U.S. Food and Drug Administration), but MoCRA, which took effect on December 29, 2023, added many new obligations. According to the FDA, cosmetic products and ingredients do not need premarket approval, with the exception of color additives.

Key obligations, based on the FDA and JETRO, are as follows.

- **Facility registration**: Register manufacturing and processing facilities with the FDA and renew every two years
- **Product listing**: List each product and its ingredients with the FDA and update annually
- **Serious adverse event reporting**: Report serious adverse events to the FDA within 15 business days of receiving them
- **Safety substantiation**: Create and keep records that substantiate the product's safety
- **Labeling**: Labels are generally in English and must include the responsible person's US contact information (a US address and a phone number or website). Claiming "FDA approved" is not allowed because it is misleading
- **Color additives**: Every color additive you use must be approved by the FDA

According to JETRO, small businesses with average US sales of less than $1 million over the past three years are exempt from facility registration and product listing. The exemption does not cover certain products, such as those that contact mucous membranes or alter appearance for more than 24 hours.

In the US, moisturizers and makeup marketed with sun-protection claims, as well as antidandruff shampoos, are both cosmetics and over-the-counter (OTC) drugs. They must meet drug requirements and carry "Drug Facts" labeling, so some Japanese cosmetics cannot be sold as they are.

## EU Cosmetics Regulations (Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009)

In the EU, the Cosmetics Regulation (1223/2009) applies across all member states, and you cannot sell cosmetics without a Responsible Person based in the EU. Even when a Japanese business sells directly to EU consumers, securing a Responsible Person is the first hurdle.

- **Responsible Person**: For products imported from outside the EU, the importer, or an EU-based person or company designated in writing by the manufacturer, acts as the Responsible Person
- **CPNP notification**: Every cosmetic product sold in the EU must be notified through the CPNP before it is placed on the market
- **Safety assessment**: Products must undergo an expert scientific safety assessment before sale
- **Product Information File (PIF)**: The Responsible Person keeps a file covering ingredients, manufacturing methods and safety data for at least 10 years after the product is placed on the market
- **Ingredients**: Lists define prohibited substances, restricted substances, and permitted colorants, preservatives and UV filters
- **Labeling**: Labels show the Responsible Person's name and EU address, the country of origin for imported products, the nominal content and the date of minimum durability, among other items
- **Animal testing**: Animal testing for cosmetic purposes is banned

## China's Cosmetics Regulations and Cross-Border Ecommerce Retail Imports

In China, selling cosmetics through regular imports requires registration or filing with the National Medical Products Administration, but under the cross-border ecommerce retail import scheme, first-import registration and filing are waived, subject to conditions. Your preparation differs completely depending on which route you choose.

### Selling Through Regular Imports

China divides cosmetics into "special cosmetics" and "general cosmetics." Special cosmetics include hair dyes, perms, spot-removing and whitening products, sunscreens, anti-hair-loss products and products claiming new functions. They require registration, which typically takes four to six months and is valid for five years. All other cosmetics are general cosmetics and require filing.

In both cases, you must appoint a company established in China as your "domestic responsible person," and that company handles the procedures. Products need Chinese labels that match the registered or filed information.

### Selling Through Cross-Border Ecommerce Retail Imports

Cross-border ecommerce retail imports let Chinese consumers buy overseas products for personal use through cross-border ecommerce channels. According to a Chinese customs announcement, goods imported by direct purchase (shipped from overseas to the consumer) or through bonded zones (customs code 1210) are regulated as personal-use items, and first-import permits, registration and filing are not required.

The following conditions apply.

- **Eligible products**: Only products on the Cross-Border E-commerce Retail Import List qualify
- **Purchase limits**: Up to 5,000 yuan per transaction and 26,000 yuan per person per year
- **Taxes**: When conditions are met, the tariff rate is 0%, and VAT and consumption tax are charged at 70% of the statutory amount
- **Local agent**: Overseas cross-border ecommerce businesses register with customs through an agent in China

If customs decides a purchase goes beyond personal use, it is treated as a regular import, and Chinese labels and prior approvals become necessary.

## Cosmetics Regulations in South Korea and Taiwan

In both South Korea and Taiwan, the basic rule is that a local importer registers or notifies the authorities and products carry labels in the local language.

### South Korea

To import and sell cosmetics in South Korea, the importing business must register as a "cosmetics responsible seller" with the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS). "Functional cosmetics," such as whitening, wrinkle-improving, tanning and UV-protection products, require an item-by-item review or report. Labeling must be in Korean (Hangul) on the container, packaging or package insert.

### Taiwan

Under Taiwan's Cosmetic Hygiene and Safety Act, manufacturers and importers must complete product registration and prepare a PIF (Product Information File) before supplying, selling or displaying a product. The PIF requirement was phased in by product type, and since July 1, 2026 it has covered almost all cosmetics, except certain handmade solid soaps.

"Specific-purpose cosmetics" designated by the authorities, such as sunscreens and hair dyes, can only be imported after obtaining a permit. Labels must be in Chinese (Traditional) or international symbols, although the full ingredient list may be in English.

Here is a summary of the main destinations by pre-market procedure, local responsible party and labeling (as of November 2026).

| Country/region | Pre-market procedure | Local responsible party | Labeling |

| EU | CPNP notification and safety assessment | EU-based Responsible Person required | Responsible Person's EU address, etc. |

## Shipping Restrictions for Cosmetics

Some cosmetics cannot be sent by international mail. Japan Post lists aerosol sprays, perfume and nail polish among items that cannot be sent to any country. Sunscreens and hair tonics cannot be sent if their alcohol content exceeds 24%.

Check whether your products can be shipped before you create product pages, so you do not end up with orders you cannot fulfill. ## Selling Different Products in Different Countries

When selling cosmetics internationally, a practical approach is to show each product only in the countries where you have confirmed the rules, rather than selling everything everywhere. For example, because sunscreens are treated as drugs in the US, you could exclude them from the US and sell them only in other countries.

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a license to sell cosmetics bought in Japan to overseas customers as they are?**

A. If you simply export cosmetics bought from a Japanese marketing authorization holder without changes, no special procedure is required in Japan. However, modifying them, such as adding local-language labels, requires a cosmetics manufacturing license and an export manufacturing notification, and accepting returns from overseas requires a cosmetics marketing authorization license. You still need to check the destination country's rules separately.

**Q. Do I need registration or filing to sell cosmetics to China through cross-border ecommerce?**

A. Under the cross-border ecommerce retail import scheme, first-import registration and filing are not required for listed products sold to individuals within the purchase limits. If you sell through regular imports, special cosmetics require registration and general cosmetics require filing.

**Q. Can I send cosmetics by international mail?**

A. Many cosmetics can be sent, but aerosol sprays, perfume and nail polish cannot go by international mail. Sunscreens and hair tonics cannot be sent if their alcohol content exceeds 24%.

**Q. Can sunscreen be sold as a cosmetic in every country?**

A. Not necessarily. In the US, products with sun-protection claims are also treated as drugs. In China they are special cosmetics, in South Korea functional cosmetics, and in Taiwan specific-purpose cosmetics, all of which involve stricter procedures than ordinary cosmetics.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

When selling cosmetics through cross-border ecommerce, check Japan's procedures, the destination country's regulations and shipping restrictions. Simply shipping Japanese cosmetics as they are requires no procedure in Japan, but the US, the EU, China, South Korea and Taiwan all require some combination of advance registration or notification, a local responsible party and local-language labeling.

Comparing countries side by side shows that how strict the rules are depends more on the type of product than on the country. Sunscreens and whitening products fall into special categories in many markets. Sorting your products by country first, then starting small with the products and markets that have lighter procedures, makes it easier to grow your overseas sales steadily. For product-specific decisions, check the latest information from each authority and consult a regulatory specialist.
