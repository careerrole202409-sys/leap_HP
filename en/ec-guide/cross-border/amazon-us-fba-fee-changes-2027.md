## Quick Overview: Amazon's 2027 US Fee Changes

If you sell on Amazon.com, FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) fulfillment fees will rise by an average of $0.19 per unit starting January 15, 2027. Amazon announced the change on November 5, 2026.

- **January 15, 2027**: FBA fulfillment fees rise by an average of $0.19 per unit, about 0.8% of the average item's selling price. Some fees go up and some go down
- **February 1, 2027**: Storage fees become a single rate based on how long inventory has been stored. Inventory that sells within six months sees flat or lower fees; aged inventory pays more
- **February 1, 2027**: The low-inventory-level fee applies only when inventory falls below 28 days of supply
- **Unchanged**: The fuel and logistics surcharge introduced in April 2026 stays the same in 2027

This article explains the changes, their impact on sellers shipping from Japan, sample calculations, and what to check before January 15, based on Amazon's official announcement. Information is current as of November 27, 2026.

## What Are Amazon's 2027 US Fee Changes?

Amazon's 2027 US fee changes are its annual update to the referral and FBA fees it charges sellers in the US store. Amazon typically announces the next year's fees in the fall, and the 2027 update was posted on its Selling Partners site on November 5, 2026, under the name of its head of Worldwide Fulfillment by Amazon.

| Date | What changes |
|---|---|
| November 5, 2026 | 2027 referral and FBA fee changes announced |
| January 15, 2027 | Most changes take effect. FBA fulfillment fees rise by an average of $0.19 |
| February 1, 2027 | Storage fee structure and low-inventory-level fee assessment change |

Amazon says the goal is to bring each fee closer to the actual cost of fulfilling that product. That means fees are not rising uniformly across all products.

## FBA Fulfillment Fees Rise by an Average of $0.19

From January 15, 2027, FBA fulfillment fees rise by an average of $0.19 per unit sold. Amazon describes this as about 0.8% of the average item's selling price.

### The Impact Depends on Product Size and Price

The increase varies by product size and price, and some fees go down. Amazon says the details for small and large standard-size items, items priced above $50, "Buy X, Get Y" promotions, Multi-Channel Fulfillment (FBA fulfillment for orders from other sales channels), and Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) are covered in its full fee change summary in Seller Central. Depending on your size tier and price band, your actual increase may differ significantly from the average.

### How 2027 Compares With 2025 and 2026

The 2027 increase is larger than in recent years.

| Fee year | Announced | FBA fee change |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | November 19, 2024 | No increase in US referral and FBA fees |
| 2026 | October 15, 2025 | Average increase of $0.08 (less than 0.5% of average selling price) |
| 2027 | November 5, 2026 | Average increase of $0.19 (about 0.8% of average selling price) |

## The Fuel and Logistics Surcharge Stays the Same in 2027

The FBA fuel and logistics surcharge (an extra charge reflecting higher fuel and logistics costs) is unchanged for 2027. The surcharge was introduced in April 2026, and Amazon says it will continue to review it as conditions change.

## Storage Fees Become Age-Based on February 1, 2027

From February 1, 2027, FBA storage fees combine into a single rate based on how long inventory has been stored. Two changes make this happen:

1. Amazon eliminates the storage utilization surcharge
2. Amazon removes the aged inventory surcharge and combines it with monthly storage fees into one age-based rate

According to Amazon, inventory that turns in under six months will see flat or lower fees, while aged inventory will see higher fees. The change favors sellers who keep fast-moving products in FBA and penalizes those with a lot of slow-moving stock.

## Changes to the Low-Inventory-Level Fee

The low-inventory-level fee is charged when FBA inventory is too low relative to sales. From February 1, 2027, it applies only when inventory falls below 28 days of supply.

The fee is currently assessed using trailing 30-day and 90-day sales. From February 1, 2027, days of supply will be based on actual shipments in the four weeks before and the four weeks after the assessment date. This means the assessment better reflects real shipment patterns, such as inventory dropping right after a temporary sales spike.

## How the Changes Affect Sellers Shipping From Japan

Sellers based in Japan can reach US customers on Amazon.com through Amazon Global Selling. With FBA, you send inventory to Amazon's fulfillment centers, and Amazon picks, packs, and ships orders and handles customer service and returns. FBA products are also eligible for Prime shipping. Japanese sellers using FBA on Amazon.com are fully affected by the 2027 changes.

### Watch Both Aged Inventory and the Low-Inventory-Level Fee

Sellers shipping from Japan to US fulfillment centers tend to face longer lead times than domestic sellers, so they often send larger batches. From February 1, 2027, however, aged inventory pays higher storage fees. Cut inventory too far, and the low-inventory-level fee applies once you drop below 28 days of supply. In 2027, finding the right shipment size between "won't go stale" and "won't drop below 28 days" matters more than ever.

Here is a rough estimate based on Amazon's published averages (actual amounts vary by product).

| Scenario | Calculation | Result |

Amazon calculates days of supply from shipments in the four weeks before and after the assessment date, so the inventory figure above is only a guide. Factor in the transit time from Japan when planning replenishment.

## What to Check Before January 15

Working through these steps before January 15, 2027 will help you understand the impact early.

1. **Check the impact by product**: Use the Fee Change Comparison dashboard in Profit Analytics in Seller Central to see fee changes at the account and product level
2. **Review your prices**: Starting with the products most affected, use the Revenue Calculator to model profit with 2027 fees, and adjust prices if needed
3. **Reduce aged inventory**: Before February 1, 2027, consider promotions, removals, or disposal for inventory likely to sit for six months or more
4. **Rework your shipment plan**: Time shipments from Japan so fast-moving products stay above 28 days of supply
5. **Read the official details**: Check the full fee change summary in Seller Central for items relevant to you, such as size tiers, items above $50, and Multi-Channel Fulfillment

## FAQ

**Q. When do Amazon's 2027 US fee changes take effect?**

A. Most changes take effect on January 15, 2027. The storage fee changes and the new low-inventory-level fee assessment start on February 1, 2027.

**Q. How much are FBA fees going up?**

A. Fulfillment fees rise by an average of $0.19 per unit, about 0.8% of the average item's selling price. The increase varies by product size and price, and some fees go down.

**Q. Is the fuel and logistics surcharge increasing?**

A. No, it is unchanged for 2027. Amazon says it will continue to review the surcharge, which it introduced in April 2026, as conditions change.

**Q. Where can I check the impact on my products?**

A. You can check it in the Fee Change Comparison dashboard in Profit Analytics in Seller Central. To model profit including fees, use the Revenue Calculator.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

An average increase of $0.19 looks small. But as Amazon itself notes, some fees go up and some go down, so what matters is the amount for each of your products, not the average. On top of that, from February 1, 2027, aged inventory pays higher storage fees, while cutting inventory too far triggers a different fee. The farther your inventory has to travel, as it does for sellers shipping from Japan, the harder it becomes to balance the two.
