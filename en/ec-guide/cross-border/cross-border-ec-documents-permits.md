## Quick Overview: Documents and Permits for Cross-Border Ecommerce

The documents and permits you need for cross-border ecommerce depend on what you sell, where you ship it, and how you ship it, not on the fact that you sell online. There are four main things to check:

1. **Domestic permits for the products you handle**: a secondhand dealer license if you buy and resell used goods, a liquor sales license if you sell alcohol, and so on
2. **Export permits**: whether your products fall under Japan's Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act (export controls) or other laws such as the Plant Protection Act or the Act on Domestic Animal Infectious Diseases Control
3. **Shipping documents**: a customs declaration label for postal items, and an export declaration with an invoice for items valued over 200,000 yen
4. **Store disclosures and laws**: Japan's Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, consumer protection and privacy rules in the destination country, and trademarks

This article organizes these four areas for small and mid-sized businesses starting cross-border ecommerce from Japan, based on public information from Japan Customs, the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), the National Tax Agency, and others. How the law applies depends on your products and how you trade, so check anything that needs a judgment call with the contacts listed later in this article.

## Overview of Documents, Permits, and Laws for Cross-Border Ecommerce

It is easiest to sort the documents, permits, and laws for cross-border ecommerce into four stages: starting the business, sourcing products, shipping overseas, and selling on your store.

| Stage | Main documents, permits, and laws | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Starting the business | Notification of business commencement (for sole proprietors) | Tax office |
| Sourcing and handling products | Secondhand dealer license, liquor sales license, etc. | Police station, tax office, etc. |
| Shipping overseas | Export license under the Foreign Exchange Act, permits under other laws, customs declaration label, export declaration, invoice | METI, relevant ministries, Japan Customs, customs brokers |
| Selling on your store | Specified Commercial Transactions Act disclosures, destination-country laws, trademarks | Consumer Affairs Agency guide, lawyers, Japan Patent Office |

If you already run an online store in Japan, the first stage is usually covered. What is new in cross-border ecommerce is mainly the shipping and selling stages.

## Documents You Need to Start Cross-Border Ecommerce

If you start cross-border ecommerce as a sole proprietor, you submit a notification of business commencement to your tax office. According to the National Tax Agency, the deadline is the tax return filing deadline for the year in which you started the business.

If you choose the blue tax return system, you also submit an application for approval of blue returns. The deadline is generally March 15 of the year you want to start filing blue returns. If you start a new business on or after January 16 of that year, the deadline is two months from the start date.

## Do You Need a Permit for Cross-Border Ecommerce?

Whether you need a permit depends on what you sell. Selling new general goods or clothing calls for different permits than selling secondhand goods or alcohol.

### Secondhand Goods: A Secondhand Dealer License

If you buy secondhand goods to resell, you need a secondhand dealer license (kobutsusho kyoka) under the Secondhand Articles Dealer Act. The Osaka Prefectural Police Q&A explains that you do not need a license to sell items you used yourself or unused items you bought for your own use, but you do need one if you buy secondhand goods to resell them.

According to the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department, you apply at the police station with jurisdiction over your main place of business, and the fee is 19,000 yen (as of November 2026).

### Alcohol: A Liquor Sales License

To sell alcohol online on an ongoing basis, you need a liquor sales license from the head of the tax office with jurisdiction over each sales location. The National Tax Agency says the license required for online liquor sales is decided case by case, based on how orders are placed and fulfilled.

Liquor sales licenses include types such as the "import/export liquor wholesale license," which allows wholesale of exported liquor. Which license you need to sell directly to overseas consumers depends on how you sell, so check with your local tax office while you are still planning.

### Food and Cosmetics: Check Rules in Japan and the Destination Country

Food, cosmetics, health foods, and pharmaceuticals may require domestic permits or notifications depending on how you manufacture or sell them, and destination countries have their own rules on ingredients, labeling, and registration. You can check domestic rules with your local public health center or prefectural office, and destination-country rules in JETRO's (Japan External Trade Organization) guide to import and export regulations, which covers about 60 major countries and regions.

## Export Permits: The Foreign Exchange Act and Other Laws

Before shipping products from Japan, check whether they require an export license or approval. This depends on the type of product and the destination country.

### Export Controls Under the Foreign Exchange Act (Security Export Control)

Japan's security export control system requires a license from the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry to export weapons, and goods or technology that could be used to develop weapons. According to METI, there are two types of controls:

- **List control**: Goods that fall under the items listed in the Export Trade Control Order require a license regardless of destination or end use, unless an exception applies
- **Catch-all control**: Even goods not on the list require a license if they could be used to develop weapons of mass destruction or conventional weapons (exports to certain countries with strict export controls are excluded)

Checking whether a product falls under list control is called a classification check (gaihi hantei). METI does not perform this check, so the exporter must do it. If you sell electronic components, machinery, or chemicals, find out whether the manufacturer can provide a classification certificate.

### Permits Under Other Laws

"Other laws" are laws other than customs laws that require permits or approvals for exports or imports. According to Japan Customs, goods that require a permit or approval under another law will not be cleared for export unless you obtain it and prove it to Customs. The main laws listed by Japan Customs are as follows (as of April 2026).

| Law | Main items |
|---|---|
| Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act (Export Trade Control Order) | Weapons, chemical weapons, narcotics, items covered by the Washington Convention (CITES), goods subject to sanctions, etc. |
| Act on Protection of Cultural Properties | Important cultural properties, important art objects, natural monuments, etc. |
| Wildlife Protection and Hunting Management Act | Wild birds and mammals, their processed products, bird eggs, etc. |
| Act on Domestic Animal Infectious Diseases Control | Cloven-hoofed animals, poultry, and their meat, sausages, ham, etc. |
| Plant Protection Act | Plants (including seeds and fruit), harmful plants, harmful animals |
| Rabies Prevention Act | Dogs, cats, raccoons, foxes, skunks |

Japan Customs states that it cannot answer questions about whether goods fall under other laws, and asks exporters to check with the ministry responsible for each law.

## Shipping Documents for Cross-Border Ecommerce

The documents you need depend on how you ship and the value of the goods. For postal items, the procedure differs depending on whether the value is 200,000 yen or less, or more than 200,000 yen.

### Shipping by Post: Customs Declaration Labels and Export Declarations

According to Japan Customs, the procedures for postal items are as follows:

1. **200,000 yen or less**: No export declaration to Customs is required. Create a customs declaration label using Japan Post's International Mail My Page service, attach it to the item, and send it. If a customs inspection finds an item that needs a permit or approval under another law, Customs will send you a postcard explaining the procedure.
2. **Over 200,000 yen**: An export declaration to Customs is required. Prepare documents such as an invoice and ask Japan Post or a customs broker to handle clearance, or file the declaration yourself.

In the value field of the customs declaration, enter the FOB value (in principle, the actual transaction amount).

### Documents Required for an Export Declaration

For an export declaration, you submit an export declaration form to Customs together with an invoice or a document in place of an invoice. According to Japan Customs, the following may also be required depending on the case:

- A packing list
- Permits or approvals under other laws (for goods covered by those laws)
- Documents related to reductions or exemptions of customs duties

If you use an international express courier, check the carrier's guidance for the documents you need.

### Items You Cannot Send by International Mail

Some items cannot be sent by international mail to any country. Japan Post lists aerosol cans, perfume, nail polish, e-cigarettes, and power banks, among others, and alcoholic beverages, sunscreen, and hair tonic are covered if their alcohol content exceeds 24%. Some countries also prohibit specific imports, so check Japan Post's country-by-country search for prohibited items.

### Keep Records for the Consumption Tax Export Exemption

Sales of goods exported from Japan are exempt from consumption tax (the export exemption). According to the National Tax Agency, you must keep documents proving the export for seven years to qualify. For postal items over 200,000 yen, the proof is the export permit. For parcels and EMS items of 200,000 yen or less, it is the acceptance receipt issued by Japan Post plus a copy of the shipping label. For more detail, see "[Consumption Tax for Cross-Border Ecommerce](/en/ec-guide/cross-border/cross-border-ec-consumption-tax/)."

## Laws Affecting Cross-Border Ecommerce: Disclosures, Privacy, and Trademarks

A cross-border online store is subject to both Japanese law and the laws of the destination country. The three areas to check first are mail-order disclosures, personal data handling, and trademarks.

### Disclosures Under the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions

The Act on Specified Commercial Transactions sets the rules for mail-order sales in Japan. According to the Consumer Affairs Agency's guide, mail-order advertising must display items such as the following:

- Selling price (shipping costs must also be shown)
- When and how payment is made
- When the goods will be delivered
- Terms for withdrawing an order or canceling the contract (including any return policy)
- The business's name, address, and phone number

If there is no return policy, buyers can cancel the contract within eight days of receiving the goods, paying the return shipping themselves. For online sales, the return policy must also appear on the final confirmation screen. If your store also sells to consumers in Japan, make sure these disclosures are in place. Which country's law applies to transactions with overseas consumers depends on the destination country's laws, so it is safest to confirm with a lawyer.

### Privacy Rules in the Destination Country

Depending on the destination country, privacy rules can apply to Japanese businesses too. JETRO explains that the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies when a company outside the EU, including in Japan, obtains personal data of people in the EU in a way that counts as "processing." If you sell to EU consumers, review your privacy policy and data handling with a specialist.

### Register Your Trademark in Each Country Where You Sell

A trademark registered in Japan does not give you rights abroad. The Japan Patent Office explains that intellectual property rights are independent in each country, and that you need to obtain trademark rights separately in each market you enter. Its subsidy program for foreign applications covers half of the cost for small and mid-sized businesses, up to 600,000 yen per trademark application (as of November 2026).

## Pre-Launch Checklist for Cross-Border Ecommerce

Before you start cross-border ecommerce, check the following:

- If you are a sole proprietor, you have submitted a notification of business commencement
- You have checked whether your products need domestic permits (secondhand dealer license, liquor sales license, etc.)
- You have checked that your products do not fall under list control or catch-all control
- You have checked that your products do not fall under other laws (Plant Protection Act, Act on Domestic Animal Infectious Diseases Control, etc.)
- You have checked that your products are not banned from international mail or prohibited in the destination country
- You have decided how to handle export declarations for orders over 200,000 yen (yourself or through a customs broker)
- You have decided how to keep export exemption records for seven years
- You have prepared Specified Commercial Transactions Act disclosures, plus terms of service and a privacy policy in the destination country's language
- You have considered filing trademarks in the countries where you sell

## FAQ

**Q. Do I need a permit to start cross-border ecommerce?**

A. Whether you need a permit depends on what you sell. You need a secondhand dealer license to buy and resell used goods, a liquor sales license to sell alcohol, and an export license or approval for goods covered by the Foreign Exchange Act or other laws. Even if you sell new general goods, confirm that your products are not regulated before you start.

**Q. Do I need an export declaration to send low-value items overseas by post?**

A. No, postal items valued at 200,000 yen or less do not require an export declaration to Customs. Create a customs declaration label using Japan Post's International Mail My Page service and attach it to the item. For items over 200,000 yen, prepare documents such as an invoice and file an export declaration.

**Q. Can individuals do cross-border ecommerce?**

A. Yes, individuals can do cross-border ecommerce. If you start as a sole proprietor, submit a notification of business commencement to your tax office. You still need to check the permits your products require and the export and shipping procedures, just as a company would.

**Q. Do I need a secondhand dealer license to sell used goods overseas?**

A. Yes, if you buy secondhand goods to resell, you need a secondhand dealer license. You do not need one to sell items you used yourself or unused items you bought for your own use. If you are unsure, ask the police station with jurisdiction over your main place of business.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

The documents and permits for cross-border ecommerce fall into four areas: domestic permits for your products, export permits, shipping documents, and store disclosures and laws. What you need is determined by the combination of product, destination, and shipping method, not by the fact that you sell online.

Laid out side by side, most of the new checks in cross-border ecommerce are concentrated in the shipping and selling stages. Put another way, narrowing down your target country and products at the start also narrows down the laws and documents you need to check. Start with one country and a small product range, finish the checks, and consult Customs or specialists on anything that needs a judgment call.
