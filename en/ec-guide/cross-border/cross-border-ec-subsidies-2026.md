## Quick Overview: Choose a Cross-Border Ecommerce Subsidy by the Costs You Want Covered

As of November 2026, there are three main national subsidies in Japan that can cover cross-border ecommerce costs: the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy, the Global Track of the New Business Expansion and Monozukuri/Commerce/Service Subsidy, and the Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy. The easiest one to use for building or upgrading a cross-border online store is the "website-related expenses" category of the Sustainability Subsidy. However, you cannot apply for website-related expenses alone, and the subsidy requested for them is capped at ¥300,000.

Application windows: the Sustainability Subsidy (Round 20) accepts applications from November 5 to December 15, 2026, 17:00, and the New Business Expansion and Monozukuri Subsidy (Round 2) from November 30, 2026, 15:00 to December 25, 2026, 15:00. For the Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy (Standard Track), the published deadline is December 1, 2026, 17:00.

This article focuses on the costs of building and running a cross-border ecommerce store. We checked the eligibility, caps, and caveats against the official application guidelines and include a worked example and application tips. For subsidies covering overseas expansion in general, such as trade shows and intellectual property, see "[Japan Overseas Expansion Subsidies 2026](/en/blog/expansion/guide-to-overseas-expansion-subsidies/)."

## What Are Subsidies for Cross-Border Ecommerce?

Subsidies for cross-border ecommerce are non-repayable funds from the national or local government that cover part of the cost of building, advertising, or translating a cross-border online store. None of the national subsidies we checked are dedicated to cross-border ecommerce. Instead, cross-border ecommerce costs are one of the eligible expense types within subsidies that support sales channel development, exporting, or IT adoption.

So when comparing subsidies, the key question is not "Is this meant for cross-border ecommerce?" but "Are the costs I want to cover (store build, ads, translation, business software, and so on) eligible?"

## 2026 List of Subsidies for Cross-Border Ecommerce

As of November 2026, the main national subsidies that can cover cross-border ecommerce costs are as follows.

| Subsidy | Main eligible cross-border ecommerce costs | Cap and subsidy rate | Application period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Business Sustainability Subsidy (General Type, Standard Track, Round 20) | Building, updating, upgrading, and running an online store (website-related expenses); online ads (publicity expenses) | ¥500,000 (up to ¥2.5M with special provisions); up to 2/3 | Applications: November 5 – December 15, 2026, 17:00 |
| New Business Expansion and Monozukuri/Commerce/Service Subsidy (Global Track, Round 2) | Building a website to promote the subsidized project, advertising, interpreting and translation | ¥25M–¥70M (up to ¥90M with the wage-increase special provision); minimum ¥7.5M; 2/3 | Applications: November 30, 2026, 15:00 – December 25, 2026, 15:00 |
| Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy (Standard Track) | IT tools registered with the secretariat (software such as order and inventory management, cloud fees) | ¥50,000 to ¥4.5M; up to 1/2 (up to 2/3 if conditions are met) | Published deadline: December 1, 2026, 17:00 |

Caps and application periods change with each round. Always check the latest application guidelines before you apply.

### Small Business Sustainability Subsidy: Use It to Build or Upgrade Your Online Store

The Small Business Sustainability Subsidy can be used directly for building a cross-border online store. The Round 20 guidelines list the cost of "developing, building, updating, upgrading, and running websites, ecommerce sites, and systems" for sales channel development as eligible "website-related expenses."

- Eligibility: small businesses (no more than 5 regular employees in commerce and services, or 20 in manufacturing and other industries)
- Cap: ¥500,000, plus ¥500,000 with the invoice special provision, ¥1.5M with the wage-increase special provision, or ¥2M with both
- Subsidy rate: up to 2/3 (up to 3/4 for loss-making businesses under the wage-increase special provision)
- Website-related expenses: cannot be the only expense in the application; the subsidy requested is capped at ¥300,000
- Publicity expenses (online ads, social media ads, and so on): cannot be the only expense in the application; the subsidy requested is capped at ¥300,000
- Schedule: the deadline to request the business support plan (Form 4) from your local chamber of commerce or society of commerce and industry is December 4, 2026. Results are expected around March 2027

Examples of eligible costs include creating or updating a website to sell products, SEO work with clear effects and scope, and producing promotional photos for the website. Website consulting fees and websites that are not up and running within the project period are not eligible.

### Global Track of the New Business Expansion and Monozukuri Subsidy: Combine It with Export Investment

The Global Track supports strengthening domestic production bases for exporting, and you can include cross-border ecommerce website and translation costs in the project. However, a website-only project does not qualify.

- Eligibility: projects that strengthen domestic production bases so the business can independently open new overseas sales channels with its own products. Projects led by business partners are not eligible
- Required costs: either machinery/system build costs or building costs
- Website: the cost of a website promoting the subsidized project falls under "advertising and sales promotion expenses," capped at 5% of projected annual sales (excluding tax) per year of the business plan period
- Interpreting and translation: eligible only in the Global Track, capped at ¥300,000 (excluding tax). Only translation needed for advertising and sales promotion is eligible
- Results: expected around March 2027

Because the minimum subsidy is ¥7.5M, a 2/3 subsidy rate means the project must have at least ¥11.25M in eligible costs. This program is better suited to manufacturers investing in export production than to small businesses just starting cross-border ecommerce.

### Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy: Use It for Ecommerce Operations Software

The Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy (formerly the IT Introduction Subsidy) is not for building an online store. It covers business software such as order processing and inventory management, and only IT tools registered with the secretariat are eligible.

- Eligible costs: software purchase, cloud fees (up to two years), and implementation costs (setup, training, maintenance support, and so on)
- Subsidy amount: ¥50,000 to under ¥1.5M for one or more business processes; ¥1.5M to ¥4.5M for four or more
- How to apply: jointly with an IT implementation support provider registered with the secretariat
- Requirements: a GBizID Prime account and a SECURITY ACTION declaration with IPA, among others
- Schedule: the published Standard Track deadline is December 1, 2026, 17:00, with grant decisions expected on January 19, 2027

Under the IT tool registration guidelines, website builder tools that include additional development costs and anything similar to advertising cannot be registered as IT tools. Consider this program if you want to streamline order processing or inventory management for your cross-border store.

### Local Government Grants: Check Your Prefecture and City

Prefectures and municipalities also offer grants that can cover marketplace listings or website production. For example, the Tokyo Metropolitan Small and Medium Enterprise Support Center's Market Development Grant covers initial ecommerce listing fees and website production or upgrade costs as sales promotion expenses (up to ¥3M, up to 1/2). However, trade show participation costs must be included in the application, and the FY2026 application window (May 15–29, 2026) has closed.

Although it is not a subsidy, JETRO's JAPAN MALL project introduces Japanese consumer products to overseas ecommerce buyers and connects them to sales on overseas ecommerce sites. Participation is free (some partners offer optional paid add-ons).

## Which Cross-Border Ecommerce Costs Are Eligible

Whether a cross-border ecommerce cost is eligible depends on the subsidy. Based on the application guidelines, the main costs break down as follows (as of November 2026).

| Cost | Sustainability Subsidy | Global Track | Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building or upgrading the online store | Eligible (website-related expenses, capped at ¥300,000) | A website promoting the subsidized project is eligible (advertising and sales promotion expenses) | Cannot be registered as an IT tool (for example, builder tools with additional development costs) |
| Online and social media ads | Eligible (publicity expenses, capped at ¥300,000) | Eligible (advertising and sales promotion expenses) | Not eligible (similar to advertising) |
| Translating product pages | Not mentioned in the guidelines (interpreting and translation tied to trade shows are eligible as trade show expenses) | Translation needed for advertising and sales promotion is eligible (capped at ¥300,000) | Not mentioned in the application or IT tool registration guidelines |
| Order and inventory management software | Software for operational efficiency is eligible (website-related expenses) | Dedicated software and information systems are eligible (machinery/system build costs) | Eligible (registered IT tools) |

Under all three subsidies, costs ordered, contracted, or paid before the grant decision are not eligible.

## Worked Example: Building a Cross-Border Store with the Sustainability Subsidy

Here is how the Sustainability Subsidy's caps work, using the conditions in the guidelines. All amounts are hypothetical and exclude tax, with no special provisions applied.

| Cost | Amount | 2/3 of the amount | Subsidy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website-related expenses (outsourcing the cross-border store build) | ¥600,000 | ¥400,000 | ¥300,000 (website-related expense cap) |
| Trade show expenses (overseas trade show booth fee) | ¥150,000 | ¥100,000 | ¥100,000 |
| Total | ¥750,000 | ¥500,000 | ¥400,000 |

In this example, the ¥300,000 cap on website-related expenses brings the subsidy to ¥400,000, leaving ¥350,000 for you to cover. Because the subsidy is paid afterward, you also need to pay the full ¥750,000 up front. A website created for ¥500,000 (excluding tax) or more counts as "property subject to disposal restrictions," which may limit using it for other purposes or discarding it for a set period (normally five years from acquisition).

The Global Track's advertising and sales promotion cap also depends on projected sales. For example, if projected annual sales over the plan period are ¥20M, the cap is 5%, or ¥1M.

## How to Apply for a Cross-Border Ecommerce Subsidy

From reading the guidelines to receiving the money, a cross-border ecommerce subsidy takes several months to over a year. Using the Sustainability Subsidy (Round 20) as an example, the steps are:

1. Get a GBizID Prime account
2. Prepare your business plan and subsidized project plan (Form 2)
3. Ask your local chamber of commerce or society of commerce and industry to issue a business support plan (Form 4) (deadline: December 4, 2026)
4. Apply through the online application system (deadline: December 15, 2026, 17:00)
5. Selection (results expected around March 2027)
6. Submit quotes and receive the grant decision (this may take about one to two months after results are announced)
7. Order and carry out the store build and other work on or after the grant decision date
8. Submit a performance report; once the amount is finalized, request and receive payment
9. Report on the situation one year after the project ends

## Tips for Applying for a Cross-Border Ecommerce Subsidy

To avoid problems, work backward from the deadline and prepare early, do not place orders before the grant decision, and plan your cash flow first.

### Get Your GBizID Prime Account Early

A GBizID Prime account is required to apply for the Sustainability Subsidy, the New Business Expansion and Monozukuri Subsidy, and the Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy. Online applications by a corporate representative are reviewed promptly, but paper applications can take up to one month.

### Do Not Order Your Store Build Before the Grant Decision

Costs ordered, contracted, or paid before the grant decision are not eligible. If you rush to commission your cross-border store before then, that cost will not be subsidized.

### Treat the Subsidy as a Reimbursement and Prepare Funds

Subsidies are paid after the project is completed and the performance report is submitted. In Round 20 of the Sustainability Subsidy, the deadline is December 15, 2026, and results are expected around March 2027. Make a cash flow plan that lets you pay the full production and advertising costs up front.

### Spell Out Who You Will Sell to and in Which Countries

The Sustainability Subsidy supports sales channel development, and the Global Track supports exporting to new overseas markets. What matters in the business plan is not building a cross-border store itself, but showing concretely which customers in which countries you will reach, how you will attract them, and how that leads to sales. The Sustainability Subsidy excludes projects that are not expected to lead to sales within about one year after the project ends.

## FAQ

**Q. Can I use a subsidy to pay for building a cross-border online store?**

A. Yes, with conditions. Under the Small Business Sustainability Subsidy, the cost of building, updating, upgrading, and running an ecommerce site for sales channel development is eligible as website-related expenses. However, you cannot apply for website-related expenses alone, and the subsidy requested is capped at ¥300,000 (Round 20 guidelines).

**Q. Can I build an online store with the Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy (formerly the IT Introduction Subsidy)?**

A. It is not designed to pay for building the store itself. Only IT tools registered with the secretariat are eligible, and the IT tool registration guidelines exclude website builder tools with additional development costs and anything similar to advertising. Think of it as a program for adopting business software for ecommerce operations, such as order and inventory management.

**Q. When will I receive the subsidy?**

A. The subsidy is paid afterward, once the project is completed and the performance report is submitted. In Round 20 of the Sustainability Subsidy, results are expected around March 2027, followed by the grant decision, the project itself, and the performance report before payment.

**Q. Can I combine multiple subsidies?**

A. You cannot receive more than one national subsidy for the same project. The Sustainability Subsidy guidelines also state that you cannot receive another national subsidy for the same subsidized project. Even when using them for different projects or costs, check with both secretariats in advance.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

In 2026, the basic rule of thumb for cross-border ecommerce costs is: the Sustainability Subsidy for building or upgrading your online store, the Global Track of the New Business Expansion and Monozukuri Subsidy when combined with export-focused capital investment, and the Digitalization and AI Adoption Subsidy for order and inventory management software. None of them are dedicated to cross-border ecommerce, so the fastest way to choose is to check whether the costs you want covered are eligible.

Another takeaway: with the Sustainability Subsidy capping website-related expenses at ¥300,000, subsidies alone are unlikely to cover all of your cross-border ecommerce costs. Treat the subsidy as funding for part of your launch, and plan for the marketing and operating costs after the store goes live. That also helps you get results after you are selected.
