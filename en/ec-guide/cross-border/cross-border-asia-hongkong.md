## [Quick Summary] Singapore, Taiwan & Hong Kong: The Ideal Markets for Your First Cross-Border E-Commerce Venture

When you first consider launching cross-border e-commerce, figuring out which market to start with can feel overwhelming. Western markets may be large, but competition is fierce and the hurdles around language support and customs are high. Singapore, Taiwan, and Hong Kong, on the other hand, offer strong consumer trust in Japanese products, robust demand, and well-developed e-commerce ecosystems — making them an ideal starting point.

This guide explains why these three markets are worth targeting (backed by data), then provides a detailed look at the major platforms — Shopee, Lazada, and Amazon Singapore — including their features and how to get started. We also map out a risk-managed roadmap from test-selling on marketplaces to building your own store with Shopify. By the time you finish reading, you'll be ready to decide exactly which marketplace to register on first.


## Why Singapore, Taiwan & Hong Kong? A Data-Backed Case for These Markets

### What All Three Markets Have in Common: A Deep Affinity for Japanese Products

The first thing to verify when planning cross-border e-commerce is whether genuine demand for your products exists in the target market. On that front, Singapore, Taiwan, and Hong Kong all stand out for their exceptionally strong affinity with Japanese products.

Taiwan consistently ranks among the top sources of inbound visitors to Japan. According to data from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), more than 4.2 million Taiwanese visited Japan in 2023. That travel experience cultivates deep trust in Japanese brands, lowering the psychological barrier to purchasing Japanese goods online — which is lower there than in almost any other market. Singapore's high income levels and widespread English proficiency mean consumers are comfortable shopping on overseas platforms. Hong Kong, likewise, has high brand trust for Japanese goods and a mature cross-border e-commerce culture.

### Low Tariff and Regulatory Hurdles

Singapore historically offered GST exemption thresholds (purchases under SGD 400 were GST-exempt prior to 2023), and even today, most consumer goods (outside agriculture and chemicals) face minimal import restrictions — making it a welcoming environment for small and mid-sized Japanese businesses. Taiwan has strong economic ties with Japan, and most product categories — food, cosmetics, daily goods — can be exported relatively smoothly. Hong Kong is essentially a zero-tariff free trade port, making it one of the most cost-effective markets to enter from a customs standpoint.

### Market Size and Growth

E-commerce across Southeast Asia is expanding rapidly, with Singapore boasting the highest per-capita e-commerce spend among ASEAN nations. Taiwan's domestic e-commerce penetration is high and mobile commerce is accelerating. Hong Kong, as Asia's business hub, sees active cross-border purchasing, particularly for Japanese products, cosmetics, and food. Taken together, all three markets offer exactly what small and mid-sized businesses need for a first cross-border venture: large enough market size, but far less intense competition than in Western markets.


## Platform Overview and How to Use Each One

### Shopee: The Top Choice for Southeast Asia and Taiwan

Shopee is an e-commerce marketplace launched in 2015 by Singapore-based Sea Limited, and now operates across Southeast Asia and Taiwan. Its monthly active users exceed 375 million (as of 2024), including over 230 million monthly visitors in Indonesia alone — giving it a dominant presence across the entire region.

For Japanese sellers, the biggest advantage is Shopee Japan, the Japanese subsidiary, which means the management dashboard is available in Japanese. There are no initial or monthly fees, and selling fees for cross-border sellers shipping from Japan are also generally waived (note: terms may vary by listing type or period — check the official site for the latest information). A shipping subsidy of approximately ¥130–300 per order is also available, making small-scale starts highly accessible.

To get started, apply through the dedicated page on Shopee Japan's official website; once approved, open your shop and list products. You select one initial market, and after listing 10 or more products, you can expand to additional markets. During major shopping events (11.11 and 12.12), many sellers report sales two to three times their normal monthly totals — all without spending on advertising.

### Lazada: Southeast Asia's Established Marketplace Under Alibaba

Lazada was founded in Singapore in 2012 and is now part of the Alibaba Group, operating in Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam. It is recognized alongside Shopee as one of the region's two dominant platforms.

What sets Lazada apart from Shopee is the strength of its logistics network, backed by Alibaba's Cainiao logistics infrastructure. Singapore and Malaysia in particular attract buyers with higher purchasing power, making it relatively easier to move higher-priced items. That said, Japanese-language support is more limited, and the onboarding process is somewhat more demanding than Shopee. For first-time cross-border sellers, we recommend starting with Shopee to get comfortable, then expanding to Lazada as a second step.

### Amazon Singapore: Reaching Affluent Consumers

Amazon.sg serves the English-speaking Singapore market. In some cases, Japanese sellers can list through Amazon Global via Amazon Seller Central Japan, but often establishing a local entity or working through a partner is required — making the process more complex than Shopee. That said, for brands with strong positioning, reaching Singapore's affluent, English-speaking consumer base can make Amazon Singapore a compelling channel.

### Qoo10: Japanese Products Shine on This Platform

Qoo10 originated in Singapore (now under eBay) and operates in Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia. In Singapore particularly, it has a steady following, and Japanese cosmetics, food, and fashion accessories sell well alongside K-pop merchandise and Korean beauty products. If you already sell on Qoo10 Japan, expanding internationally is relatively straightforward.

### Shopify: Building Your Own Store to Maximize Margin and Brand

Selling on marketplaces is ideal for a small-scale start, but comes with commission fees and limited brand control. Once you have accumulated sufficient sales data and customer insights, building a Shopify-powered branded store in parallel lets you improve margins while expressing your brand identity freely. Shopify has multilingual and multi-currency support built in, making it relatively easy to build English- and Traditional Chinese-language stores targeting Singapore, Taiwan, and Hong Kong.


## Real-World Cross-Border E-Commerce Success Stories

### Case 1: Mikihouse (Children's Clothing & Baby Products)

Mikihouse (Sanki Shoji Co., Ltd.), headquartered in Osaka, has established operations across Asia — including Taiwan, Singapore, and Hong Kong — selling its premium children's and baby products. The brand's reputation for Japanese quality and safety resonates strongly with parents across Asia, and it has built a loyal following among consumers who have visited Japan. Its multi-channel strategy, combining domestic and international e-commerce, offers a model that smaller businesses can adapt.

### Case 2: Kose Corporation (Cosmetics)

Kose Corporation has been active in Asian cross-border e-commerce, including through Amazon Singapore, with brands like SEKKISEI earning strong recognition across the region. The trust placed in Japanese cosmetics for their quality and ingredient standards means Japanese beauty brands can command pricing power in these markets without getting caught in price wars against cheaper alternatives — an approach smaller cosmetics brands can replicate.

### Case 3: Small Japanese Food Manufacturers on Shopee

According to case studies published by ebiz (run by Japan's Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation), a number of small Japanese food and confectionery manufacturers have built steady sales through Shopee in Southeast Asia, selling snacks, condiments, and health foods. Japanese food products that are hard to find in local supermarkets — specialty regional items and niche food products — have proven particularly popular in Singapore, Malaysia, and Taiwan. Many of these sellers focus their inventory push around Shopee's major sale events.


## From Small-Scale Start to Your Own Store: A Step-by-Step Roadmap

### Step 1: Market Research and Product Selection (Months 1–2)

Start by identifying which of your product categories have demand in your target markets. Even a quick look at Shopee's search results and bestseller rankings gives you a clear picture of which categories are growing. Also study the pricing and product listing strategies of local and Chinese competitor sellers.

### Step 2: Small-Scale Launch on Shopee (Months 3–6)

Apply for an account via Shopee Japan, and select Singapore or Taiwan as your initial market. Begin with 10–30 products and test which items, price points, and listing styles generate the best response. At this stage, rely on Shopee's organic traffic and major sale events rather than paid advertising. Japan Post's international small parcel and EMS (Express Mail Service) are the most practical shipping options; take advantage of Shopee's shipping subsidy as well.

### Step 3: Expanding to Additional Markets (Months 6–12)

Once you've achieved consistent sales in your first market, consider expanding on Shopee to additional markets (Malaysia, Thailand, etc.) and listing on Lazada and Qoo10. This is also a good time to start building a multilingual Shopify store. Your own branded site pairs well with retargeting ads (Facebook, Instagram) aimed at users who discovered you on the marketplace, enabling you to pursue higher customer lifetime value (LTV).

### Step 4: Direct Sales Through Your Own Shopify Store (Month 12+)

Use the sales data, customer insights, and bestselling product knowledge you gathered from marketplace selling to build a full cross-border store on Shopify. At this stage, invest in rich content in local languages (English, Traditional Chinese), and integrate with social media marketing and SEO to build your brand as a long-term asset. Think of the split as: "marketplaces for acquiring new customers, your own site for cultivating repeat buyers."


## FAQ: Common Questions from Cross-Border E-Commerce Teams

### Q1. Should I Start with Singapore or Taiwan?

For consumer goods like cosmetics, food, or daily necessities, Taiwan tends to be slightly more accessible — Japanese communication styles feel familiar there, and many Taiwanese consumers have personal experience visiting Japan. On the other hand, if your team is comfortable with English, or you're selling IT products, electronics, or premium brands, Singapore's higher purchasing power and e-commerce maturity make it a strong starting point. Since Shopee allows you to operate in both markets simultaneously, a practical approach is to focus on one first, build your expertise, then expand.

### Q2. What Product Categories Sell Well Through Cross-Border E-Commerce?

The most in-demand Japanese product categories in Southeast Asia, Taiwan, and Hong Kong are cosmetics and skincare (strong trust in Japanese quality), food and confectionery (valued for their authenticity and rarity), baby and children's products (safety-focused consumers), health foods and supplements, and kitchen and household goods. These categories are well-positioned to compete on the "Made in Japan" brand value rather than price alone. Be cautious with generic products where local equivalents are available at low prices, and always verify import regulations for food additives and quasi-drug products before shipping.

### Q3. How Should I Handle Logistics and Shipping?

For a small-scale start, Japan Post's international mail services — small parcel, SAL (surface air lifted), and EMS (Express Mail Service) — offer the best cost-performance balance. When selling through Shopee, the platform's built-in logistics service is also available, and delivery to Taiwan and Singapore tends to be relatively reliable. As sales grow, move to a third-party logistics (3PL) provider for warehousing and fulfillment to reduce delivery times and costs. Always verify each country's import regulations and ingredient restrictions for food and cosmetics before shipping.


## Summary: Starting Cross-Border E-Commerce Means Choosing One Market and One Platform

Singapore, Taiwan, and Hong Kong combine three essential qualities: high consumer trust in Japanese products, mature e-commerce ecosystems, and low tariff hurdles — making them the ideal first markets for cross-border e-commerce. And the easiest entry point into all three is a small-scale start on Shopee. While Japanese-language support, no listing fees, reduced commission rates, and major sale traffic are in your favor, start listing in one market and let the data accumulate.

In parallel, building a multilingual Shopify store reduces your marketplace dependency, strengthens your brand, and transforms cross-border selling from a side experiment into a full-scale business. The key isn't waiting until everything is perfect — it's making a small, low-risk start and improving as you learn.
