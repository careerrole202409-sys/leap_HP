## Quick Overview: What You Need to Know Before Using LINE for E-Commerce

LINE has one of the highest open rates of any messaging app in Japan, which makes it a channel e-commerce sellers can't afford to ignore when it comes to building repeat customers. This guide covers the basics of running a LINE Official Account for business, how to implement segmented messaging using purchase history, customer attributes, and behavioral data (through ID linking and the LINE Tag), and how to maximize open and click rates with rich messages, rich menus, and coupons. We'll walk through real company examples along the way, plus practical guidance on send frequency, A/B testing, and how LINE fits alongside email marketing.

## LINE Is a Channel E-Commerce Sellers Can't Afford to Skip

### Where Friend Growth Starts: Notification Messages and Digital Membership Cards

LINE's "notification message" feature is one of the most effective ways to grow your friend base. Apparel brand nairo reported that roughly one in three users who received a purchase or shipping notification through LINE went on to add the brand as a friend. Sporting goods giant Mizuno takes a different approach with a digital membership card — customers scan a QR code and both the membership card issuance and the friend addition complete in under five seconds. The common thread in both cases: prompting friend additions at a moment that feels natural to the customer.

## Implementing Segmented Messaging

### Capturing Behavioral Data Through ID Linking and the LINE Tag

To run segmented messaging, start by installing the LINE Tag on your website so you can capture behavioral data — site visits, purchases, items added to cart. That data lets you identify shoppers who abandoned a cart or customers who've purchased before, and reach out to them individually. For even more granular segmentation, connecting to an external DMP (data management platform) or an official service like LINE's reservation feature lets you unify online and offline data for more sophisticated targeting.

### The Impact of Post-Purchase Sequences and Cart Abandonment Messaging

The impact of segmented messaging shows up clearly in reported results. Cosmetics brand Beautiful Skin Online Shop tripled conversions attributed to LINE after implementing a post-purchase message sequence. Cart abandonment messaging through LINE has also been reported to achieve roughly seven times the conversion rate compared to email. That gap reflects just how well LINE's high open rate pairs with reaching a shopper right at the moment they've dropped off.

## Maximizing Click-Through With Rich Messages and Rich Menus

### Lens Direct's Two-Tab Rich Menu

Contact lens retailer Lens Direct reported roughly a 15% increase in both site visits and orders after implementing a two-tab rich menu. Paired with auto-login, it lets friends jump straight from a single tap to a seamless checkout flow. Rich messages and card-type messages — which combine images with multiple buttons — also tend to be more visually engaging than plain text, which typically translates into higher click-through rates.

### Using Coupon Distribution

LINE Official Account's coupon feature lets you send friend-exclusive discount coupons. Pairing a coupon with post-purchase follow-up or a win-back message for dormant customers makes it easier to give shoppers a concrete reason to buy again.

## Practical Guidance on Send Frequency and A/B Testing

Send too often, and you risk getting blocked; send too rarely, and you lose visibility. A reasonable starting point is one to two messages a week, adjusting frequency from there based on open rate, click-through rate, and block rate. Running ongoing A/B tests — varying message copy and send times — is also worth doing continuously to sharpen your messaging over time.

## Designing Your Overall Strategy Alongside Email

LINE and email each play to different strengths. LINE's high open rate and immediacy make it well suited to time-sensitive moments — cart abandonment follow-up, coupon distribution, post-purchase notifications. Email, on the other hand, is better suited to detailed product information or showcasing multiple products at once. Rather than treating them as competing channels, the key to designing your overall LINE-and-e-commerce strategy is matching each channel to the right customer touchpoint. It's likely more teams will handle this kind of messaging design and performance review through ongoing conversation with AI going forward.

## FAQ

**Q. Where should I start if I want to grow my LINE friend base?**

A. Start by designing prompts to join at moments that feel natural to the customer — right after a purchase completes, or when a shipping notification goes out. As nairo's example shows, giving customers a concrete reason to want the notification lowers the barrier to adding you as a friend.

**Q. What do I need in place before I can start segmented messaging?**

A. The first step is installing the LINE Tag on your site so you can capture behavioral data — visits, purchases, items added to cart. From there, define the specific segments you want to target, whether that's shoppers who abandoned a cart or customers who've purchased before.

**Q. Should I prioritize LINE or email?**

A. You don't need to pick one over the other. Use LINE for time-sensitive messaging like cart abandonment follow-up or limited-time coupons, and use email when you need to share detailed product information or showcase multiple products at once — matching the channel to the purpose works best.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

A LINE Official Account lets you design an entire repeat-purchase flow — from growing your friend base through notification messages and digital membership cards, to segmented messaging via the LINE Tag, to driving purchases with rich messages and coupons — all within a single channel. As the nairo, Mizuno, and Lens Direct examples show, results don't come from simply sending messages — they come from delivering information at a moment and in a context that feels natural to the customer.
