## Quick Overview: What You Need to Know Before Setting Up HubSpot for E-Commerce

Plenty of people assume HubSpot is a B2B-only tool. In reality, it works well for e-commerce sites too — particularly for high-ticket products, subscription models, and D2C brands. This guide breaks down why cart abandonment is such a persistent challenge for e-commerce, then walks through building a three-step automated cart abandonment scenario in HubSpot (1 hour → 24 hours → 48 hours after abandonment). We'll also cover repeat-purchase scenario templates — welcome series for new customers, post-purchase follow-up, and win-back campaigns for dormant customers — plus how to use A/B testing to optimize subject lines and send times.

## E-Commerce Cart Abandonment and What HubSpot Can Do About It

### Cart Abandonment Runs Around 70% — and Follow-Up Email Has Strong ROI

E-commerce cart abandonment rates typically run around 70%. In other words, most shoppers who add something to their cart end up leaving the site without completing the purchase. Cart abandonment follow-up email addresses that drop-off without requiring additional ad spend — it reaches people who are already warm leads — making it one of the highest-ROI tactics available. Connecting HubSpot (Marketing Hub) to your e-commerce site lets you automate that follow-up, segment your audience, and drive repeat purchases to maximize customer lifetime value (LTV).

## Building a Three-Step Cart Abandonment Scenario in HubSpot

### Connecting Your E-Commerce Site to HubSpot

Start by connecting your e-commerce platform — Shopify, for example — to HubSpot so that purchase and cart data flows into HubSpot. That connection lets you use "added to cart but didn't purchase" behavior as a trigger to automatically kick off your email scenario.

### The 1-Hour, 24-Hour, and 48-Hour Sequence

A basic cart abandonment scenario runs in three steps. First, within an hour of items being added to the cart, send a follow-up email nudging the shopper toward completing the purchase. Second, if the cart is still abandoned after 24 hours, send a reminder email with a limited-time offer attached. Third, send a final notice email at 48 hours, after which the scenario ends regardless of outcome. This staged approach nudges shoppers toward completing a purchase without feeling pushy.

## Repeat-Purchase Scenario Templates

### A Welcome Series for New Customers

For first-time buyers, design a multi-email welcome series that introduces your brand's story and shows customers how to use what they bought. Deepening a customer's connection to the brand while their excitement is still fresh right after purchase sets up future repeat purchases.

### Post-Purchase Follow-Up and Win-Back Campaigns for Dormant Customers

After purchase, design follow-up emails that reinforce the experience — usage tips timed around delivery, a review request after a set period has passed. It's also worth setting up a win-back scenario that sends a re-engagement offer to customers who haven't purchased in a while, so you're not leaving revenue from your existing customer base on the table.

## Using A/B Testing to Optimize Subject Lines and Send Times

HubSpot's A/B testing features let you send variations of subject lines and send times to see which combinations drive better open and click rates. Building the scenario once isn't the end of the work — running these tests continuously and refining the scenario itself over time is what keeps your email marketing performance consistent.

## Moving Past the "HubSpot Is for B2B" Assumption

HubSpot tends to be a particularly good fit for high-ticket e-commerce products, subscription-based e-commerce, B2B wholesale, and D2C brands. For low-ticket, high-transaction-volume categories like everyday consumer goods, a dedicated e-commerce marketing tool may be a better fit. In fact, brands like Anker Japan and Hamee appear among HubSpot's listed customers — a sign that adoption extends well beyond B2B companies into brand-driven e-commerce and D2C businesses. It's likely we'll see more teams handling this kind of scenario design and performance review through ongoing conversation with AI going forward.

## FAQ

**Q. Is connecting HubSpot to an e-commerce platform difficult?**

A. Most e-commerce platforms, Shopify included, offer a dedicated app or built-in feature for connecting to HubSpot. The connection itself is typically completed through admin panel settings, without requiring deep development expertise.

**Q. How many cart abandonment emails should I send?**

A. A common baseline is three: at 1 hour, 24 hours, and 48 hours. Sending too many can come across as pushy and backfire. Start with the three-step sequence, then adjust based on your open and click rates.

**Q. Is HubSpot still worth it for a store selling low-ticket products?**

A. HubSpot tends to fit best with high-ticket products, subscription models, and D2C brands. For low-ticket, high-transaction-volume categories like everyday consumer goods, a dedicated e-commerce marketing tool may serve you better. It's worth weighing your product mix and average order value before deciding.

## Summary and Key Takeaway

With e-commerce cart abandonment rates running around 70%, follow-up email to reduce that drop-off isn't optional — it's essential. HubSpot lets you automate not just the "1 hour → 24 hours → 48 hours" cart abandonment sequence, but a full repeat-purchase flow that includes welcome series and win-back campaigns for dormant customers. What that process reveals is that sending emails isn't the goal in itself — the real value comes from continuously refining the scenario through A/B testing and nurturing the customer relationship over time.
