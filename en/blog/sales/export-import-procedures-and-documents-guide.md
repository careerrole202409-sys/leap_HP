## Why are trade documents so complex? The reasons and their importance

### Growth opportunity and the first barrier in international trade
International trade is a great growth opportunity for Japanese SMEs. However, for beginners, complex procedures and many unfamiliar documents often become the first big barrier. This complexity can feel overwhelming when entering new markets.

In fact, each of these procedures and documents is tied to international rules and the laws of each country, and they each have an important meaning.

### Serious risks caused by document errors
Do not underestimate "just documents." Errors or missing information in trade documents can lead to far more serious outcomes than you expect. For example, customs delays, unexpected extra charges such as storage or demurrage, and in the worst case, cargo seizure or legal problems.

Many failures stem from lack of knowledge or mistakes during preparation. Accurate document preparation is not mere paperwork — it is the foundation for smooth and trouble-free international trade.

## Understand the basic flow of export and import

### Step-by-step export process
Exporting goods overseas can be divided into several steps. First, find a reliable overseas partner and sign a contract. Then confirm whether export permission is required from agencies like the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, especially for items subject to list control or catch-all regulations.

Next, pack the goods for international transport and book cargo space with a shipping company or airline, often through a forwarder. Deliver the cargo to a bonded area, submit the required documents such as invoices and packing lists to customs, and file the export declaration. After customs review and inspection, if export permission is granted, load the goods and obtain documents such as a bill of lading (B/L). Finally, collect payment according to the contract.

### Step-by-step import process
Importing goods also requires careful planning. Decide what to import, when, and under what conditions, then place the order. Depending on the goods, import approvals under domestic laws such as the Food Sanitation Act or Plant Protection Act may be required, so prior confirmation is essential.

When the exporter ships the goods, you will receive shipping documents such as the B/L and invoice. When the cargo arrives in Japan and enters a bonded area, file the import declaration electronically through systems such as NACCS. After customs review, inspection, and payment of customs duties and consumption tax, import permission is issued and you can take delivery of the goods.

### Time required and key stakeholders
The time required for export/import procedures varies widely depending on factors such as the type of cargo, document accuracy, and whether customs inspection is required. For air freight, customs clearance can take as little as 0.3 hours in a smooth case with no document problems.

International trade involves many parties, including exporters, importers, customs brokers, forwarders, shipping lines or airlines, banks, insurance companies, customs authorities, and agencies like METI. Smooth coordination among these parties is the key to successful trade.

## Essential trade document guide you should know

### Commercial invoice
The commercial invoice is one of the most important documents issued by the exporter. It serves as the invoice, delivery note, and statement of goods.

Import customs authorities use it to determine customs valuation and calculate duties. It should include exporter and importer information, invoice number and date, vessel name, payment terms, Incoterms, goods details (product names, quantity, unit price, amount, and HS code recommended), country of origin, and signature. Even for free samples, indicate the market value and label them as "No Commercial Value." This is crucial for customs procedures.

### Packing list
A packing list details the contents of the shipment. It describes which goods are packed in each package, the weight and volume of each package, and shipping marks.

Price information is usually not included. It is essential for customs inspections and for the importer to verify received cargo. The contents of the invoice and packing list — especially product names, quantity, and shipping marks — must match exactly.

### Certificate of origin
A certificate of origin proves the country where the goods were produced. It is a public document required for import clearance in the destination country and for preferential tariff treatment under EPAs/FTAs.

General certificates of origin are issued by chambers of commerce, while EPA-specific certificates are generally issued by a Japanese chamber of commerce. Consistency with the commercial invoice is essential, and for EPA cases, it is critical to confirm HS codes and origin rules.

### Bill of lading (B/L) and airway bill (AWB)
A bill of lading is used in maritime transport. It serves as evidence of the transport contract, cargo receipt, and as a negotiable document representing ownership.

An airway bill is used in air transport. It also serves as evidence of the transport contract and cargo receipt, but it is not negotiable. This is the key difference: a B/L can be endorsed, while an AWB cannot. This distinction matters in letters of credit transactions.

### Marine insurance policy
A marine insurance policy is the certificate of an insurance contract that covers economic loss caused by dangers during transport, such as sinking, fire, or theft.

Under CIF or CIP, the seller arranges insurance, while under FOB or CFR, the buyer is responsible for insurance. The insured amount is generally 110% of the CIF price, and you should confirm the insurance coverage terms such as Institute Cargo Clauses (A), (B), or (C).

### Export and import permits
Not all goods are freely exportable or importable. From the perspective of national security (list-controlled items under foreign exchange laws), public health (Food Sanitation Act), or environmental protection (CITES), certain goods require government permission or approval in advance. Export and import permits prove that these requirements have been met. Unauthorized export or import can result in severe penalties, so prior confirmation is indispensable.

## Pitfalls beginners often fall into and how to avoid them

### Document errors and mistakes, and their impact
The most common and impactful issues are inconsistencies between the invoice and packing list, discrepancies between the B/L and contract, and using the wrong format for the certificate of origin. These can cause customs delays, extra costs, cargo holds, and damage to your reputation with trading partners.

Establishing a double-check system and asking a specialist to review documents are effective countermeasures.

### Risks from HS code misclassification
Incorrect HS classification can change the duty rate and unexpectedly increase customs duties, or lead to additional taxes and fines for under-declaration.

HS classification requires expertise, so if you are not confident, consult a customs broker or use customs pre-classification services. METI’s cargo and technology matrix tables are also useful references.

### Misunderstanding Incoterms
Incoterms define the allocation of transport costs and transfer of risk. Misunderstanding them can cause disputes over shipping costs, insurance premiums, and liability for accidents during transport.

Before concluding a contract, clearly agree on which Incoterms rule applies (Incoterms® 2020) and the named place. Specify these details in the contract.

### Case examples: Document problems and resolutions
One exporter had a mismatch between the invoice and the actual quantity of goods, so import customs in the destination country held the cargo temporarily. After urgently sending a corrected invoice and filing an amended declaration, the goods were released after a few days, but the delay caused extra time and effort.

In another case, the B/L shipment date exceeded the latest shipment date specified in the letter of credit, so the bank rejected the documents and payment was delayed significantly. Many of these problems can be prevented with prior verification and accurate document preparation.

## Tips for making trade document preparation more efficient

### Use templates
Many trade documents have no unified format, so it is common to use templates created in Excel or other tools. Forwarders and logistics providers often supply invoice and packing list templates as part of their service. These are typically practical, but it is also important to customize them to your own business and trading partner requirements.

### Use electronic filing and online services
Most customs procedures in Japan can be handled electronically through NACCS (Nippon Automated Cargo and Port Consolidated System). This eliminates the need to submit documents at a counter and saves time and cost.

Courier online tools such as DHL’s MyDHL+ allow you to create and submit commercial invoices online. Recently, services like Logimeets offer free invoice creation tools. These digital services can streamline the process.

### The future of document preparation with AI
AI technology is bringing innovation to trade document preparation. Automating data extraction with AI-OCR, verifying consistency between documents, and compliance checking can significantly save time, reduce human error, and cut costs.

For example, companies like Omron have used AI in customs classification work to reduce the workload of routine tasks. Leap’s platform also supports overseas expansion with AI-powered strategy proposals and document management.

### Case studies: SMEs improving document preparation and efficiency
AI adoption is not limited to large corporations. In addition to Omron, many SMEs have used support from JETRO and chambers of commerce for guidance on document types and preparation methods, enabling them to start exports smoothly. Leveraging external support is also a key point in improving efficiency.

## Trusted consultation sources when you need help

### Customs (customs counselors and pre-classification services)
Customs offices across Japan have customs counselors who provide free consultations on export/import procedures and HS classification. The advance classification service lets you ask customs about the classification of goods before import, which helps you accurately estimate import costs.

### JETRO and chambers of commerce
JETRO (Japan External Trade Organization) supports SMEs with overseas expansion and offers programs such as the "New Export Support Program" for expert counseling and hands-on support. Their website also contains extensive trade and investment Q&A.

Chambers of commerce support local businesses by issuing general certificates of origin, providing trade consultations, and organizing seminars.

### Customs brokers and forwarders
Customs brokers and forwarders are specialists who handle export/import declarations, duty payment, transport arrangements, and document preparation support. Outsourcing complex procedures allows you to focus on your core business and prevents problems through expert advice.

Choose a partner with experience in your product category and trade destination, and one who is willing to provide attentive support.
