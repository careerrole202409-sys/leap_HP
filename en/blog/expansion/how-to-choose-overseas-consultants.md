## The Key to Going Global: Choosing the Right Partner — In 60 Seconds

Overseas expansion confronts you with challenges that domestic business never does: market research, legal regulations, business customs, language barriers. Overcoming all of these alone is far from easy. This is where overseas expansion consultants come in, bringing specialist knowledge and local networks to support you from strategy through execution.

But choose the wrong consultant and you'll find yourself paying a fortune for results that never materialize. This article gives you everything you need: what consultants actually provide, how much it costs, what to look for in a great partner, red flags to watch for, and how to build the most effective team by combining consultants with lawyers and accountants. Think of it as your navigation guide for the journey ahead.

## What Does an Overseas Expansion Consultant Do? Services and Costs

"Overseas expansion consultant" is a broad term covering many different service profiles. The first step in finding the right one is understanding what you actually need.

### What Consultants Typically Provide

**Market research and analysis:** Thorough investigation of market size, competitive landscape, and consumer needs in target countries. Consultants with strong local networks can uncover intelligence you couldn't find on your own.

**Strategy and business planning:** Developing market entry strategies and business plans optimized for local conditions.

**Legal and regulatory advice:** Expert guidance on local laws, permits, tax systems, and labor regulations — areas where specialized knowledge is non-negotiable.

**Partner identification and introductions:** Finding local distributors, suppliers, or joint venture partners. One brewer, Bear Ale Brewing, received a list of importers and on-the-ground market intelligence through JETRO — an example of consultant-facilitated partner discovery.

**Business launch support:** Setting up local entities, opening offices, and supporting initial hiring.

**Marketing and sales support:** Developing and executing effective marketing strategies for local markets.

The services you need will depend on your situation and which phase of expansion you're in. Know clearly what stage you're at and what kind of support you're actually looking for.

### How Much Does It Cost?

Consultant fees vary widely depending on contract structure, project scope, and the consultant's expertise. Common billing models include:

- **Monthly retainer:** Ongoing advisory relationship, typically ¥100,000–¥500,000 per month
- **Project-based:** One-time fee for a defined scope; varies widely
- **Hourly:** Spot consulting, typically ¥5,000–¥200,000 per hour
- **Success fee:** Compensation tied to measurable outcomes

The right question isn't just "how much?" but "what value do I get for that?" And remember: consultant fees are just the beginning. Budget separately for market research costs, legal fees, and travel.

Subsidies are sometimes available to offset costs, but don't plan around them — build a solid business case first, and treat public support as a bonus.

## How to Choose the Right Consultant — Red Flags and Green Lights

To avoid the trap of paying a lot and getting little in return, you need to evaluate candidates carefully.

### Industry and Regional Expertise: The Most Critical Factor

The most important thing is whether the consultant has deep expertise in your specific industry and target region. Ask for proof:

- **Sector and regional expertise:** Do they genuinely understand your industry's specific challenges and the cultural, legal, and business environment of the country you're targeting?
- **Verifiable success stories:** Ask for concrete case studies and client references. If possible, speak directly with past clients. Transparency about outcomes is a strong positive signal; vagueness is a warning.
- **Right size match:** A consultant who specializes in Fortune 500 companies may not be the right fit for an SME. Look for someone whose experience aligns with your scale and stage.

### Transparency, Communication, and Cultural Fit

Beyond expertise, the working relationship matters enormously for a long-running project.

- **Transparency:** Are they open about how they work, what progress they're making, and what obstacles they've encountered?
- **Communication quality:** How often do they report? Do they respond promptly? Who will actually be your day-to-day contact?
- **Cultural alignment:** Do their working style and values feel compatible with your company's culture? First impressions from initial meetings matter.

Online reviews can be one data point, but take them with a grain of salt — negative reviews often reflect misaligned expectations or poor communication rather than fundamental incompetence. Gather information from multiple sources.

### Red Flags to Watch For

Be cautious if you notice any of these:

- **Unverifiable results or vague claims:** Can't provide specific examples or evidence to support their claims.
- **Excessive promises or guarantees of success:** Overseas expansion has no guarantees. Anyone who promises one doesn't understand the business.
- **High-pressure sales tactics:** Rushing you toward signing, not giving you time to think.
- **Opaque fee structures:** Unclear or vaguely defined pricing.
- **Poor communication:** Slow to respond; doesn't give direct, clear answers.

If you see any of these, slow down — seek another opinion or look for other candidates.

## Getting the Most from Your Consultant

Even a great consultant won't deliver if the relationship and project management aren't working well.

### Get the Contract Right

The contract is the foundation of the whole project. Leave nothing ambiguous.

- **Scope of work (SOW):** Define specifically what tasks and responsibilities are included.
- **Deliverables:** What concrete outputs do you expect — reports, strategy documents, distributor lists?
- **Schedule and milestones:** Set specific deadlines for each key deliverable.
- **Payment terms:** Define fees, payment schedule, and currency. For international work, clarify who bears exchange rate risk.
- **Non-disclosure agreement (NDA):** Define how confidential information will be handled.

Written contracts aren't just protection against disputes — they're the mechanism for aligning mutual understanding from the start.

### Stay Actively Involved

Your consultant is an outside support resource, not a delegee for decision-making. Delegating entirely means you don't accumulate internal knowledge — and when the engagement ends, you'll have nothing to show for it.

- **Keep your team engaged:** Actively involve internal staff in consultant processes so they understand what's happening.
- **Document processes:** Ask the consultant to document their workflows and methodologies so knowledge stays in your organization.
- **Regular progress reviews:** Meet regularly to review progress, and give active feedback. Early problem detection allows for timely course correction.

Over-relying on your consultant creates two risks: when the engagement ends, you may not be able to run the business yourself; and there's always a risk that proprietary information could leave the organization. Stay in the driver's seat.

## Building Your Full Expert Team: Working with Lawyers and Accountants

Consultants are just one part of the expert team you need for overseas expansion. Legal and accounting specialists are indispensable.

### The Role of a Lawyer

Overseas operations require full compliance with local law. Contract drafting and review, local entity formation, intellectual property protection, employment law — a qualified local attorney is essential.

In markets where written contracts are standard (which is nearly everywhere internationally), inadequate contract language can escalate into serious disputes. Always consult a lawyer before making any commitments and before signing anything.

### The Role of an Accountant / Tax Adviser

International taxation, local accounting standards, transfer pricing — the accounting and tax dimensions of overseas operations are highly complex. Financial management failures and compliance violations can threaten the viability of the entire operation. For M&A transactions, financial due diligence; for operating entities, appropriate tax structuring: both require qualified professional advice.

### Making the Expert Team Work

With a lead overseas expansion consultant at the center, coordinating with lawyers and accountants creates a powerful support structure.

- **Clear roles and responsibilities:** Define who owns what to avoid information gaps and duplication.
- **Efficient communication:** Establish regular meetings and information-sharing protocols to keep everyone aligned.
- **Client-led coordination:** Ultimately, your company must verify that each specialist's advice is consistent with the overall strategy and make final decisions yourself.

A strong consultant will typically have a network of trusted local lawyers and accountants. When evaluating a consultant, ask about their professional network — this is often a useful signal of their depth of experience.

## Overseas Expansion Consultant FAQ

**Q1. What should I budget for an overseas expansion consultant?**
As a rough guide: hourly rates range from ¥5,000–¥200,000; monthly retainer agreements typically run ¥100,000–¥500,000. These vary significantly by project scope, content, and consultant expertise. Get quotes from multiple candidates and compare against the specific services offered. Also confirm upfront what additional costs may be incurred (on-site research, legal work, etc.).

**Q2. How do I spot a consultant who is weak or operating in bad faith?**
Watch for: claims of a strong track record that they can't back up with specifics; excessive promises or guaranteed success; high-pressure sales; opaque pricing; and poor communication quality. These are all warning signs.

**Q3. Is it okay to hand everything over to the consultant?**
No. Full delegation is a mistake. The consultant is a support resource; your company must remain the decision-making authority. Over-reliance means no internal expertise accumulates, and you won't be able to operate once the engagement ends. Stay actively involved, and use the engagement as a learning opportunity.
